## 0. Structure-robust, and model-dependent

Phase 5 fitted the paper's model to records generated by both published PK/PD structures and
asked what survives. The cut below is the one to read first, because the paper's claims fall
on both sides of it and the sides behave differently.

Naming, to avoid a trap: `PUB_A` is the turnover structure (the marker is a state whose
production the therapy inhibits), `PUB_B` is Attarwala's algebraic one (the marker is a
direct saturating function of the enzyme, with no state). Neither is `src/model_A/`.

### Structure-robust: holds under the paper's model AND both published structures

**The three-number bound, in all three structures EXAMINED.** [proven, for a stated class]
At a constant recorded load, all a record contains is the function from increment to marker,
and in all three structures that function belongs to a family of dimension exactly three:
`Delta(m) = A/m+B+Cm` for the paper, `(P Delta+U)/(Delta+V)` for both published ones. Four or
five parameters against three numbers is why every structure here is one short at a constant
load.

**It is not a general theorem and is not claimed as one.** The class it is proved for is
*four-monomial*: structures whose fixed-load `(Delta, m)` graph is the zero set of a
polynomial with at most four monomials. Within that class the bound is elementary, since the
polynomial is defined only up to scale. The content is entirely in MEMBERSHIP, verified for
these three and claimed for no others: `PAPER` uses `{1, m, m^2, Delta*m}`, both published
structures use `{1, m, Delta, Delta*m}`. **`PUB_A`'s full model leaves the class** the moment
its transient is used, because a marker with a state does not put `(Delta(t), m(t))` on any
algebraic curve, and that is exactly where its fourth number comes from. The class boundary
and the mechanism are the same line.

**A period-locked record is exactly singular in `K_m`.** [measured] A record whose samples all
sit at one dose-cycle phase leaves the `K_m` profile flat across the whole scan, for **every**
generator and **both** calibration branches of each. The exactly singular case is not an
artefact of the paper's rational parametrisation.

**Breaking the period lock bounds `K_m`.** [measured] Under the paper's model and under both
branches of `PUB_B`, monthly and phase-spread records give a bounded `K_m` interval, in the
same direction and by a comparable factor.

**The schedule ranking.** [measured] Monthly ~ phase-spread < 14.5 d < 14 d, by
`sd(ln theta)`, under the paper's model and both `PUB_B` branches, the two recommended
designs agreeing to about 3% across structures (`0.1893`, `0.1894`, `0.1952` on a monthly
record). **Every design conclusion in this paper is on this side of the cut**, with one
scope limit below.

**The capacity ceiling's cancellation.** [proven] The used fraction is
`frac*(1 + Delta_peak/theta)`, so `theta` and the model block both cancel and only the ratio
matters. It binds at `Delta_peak/theta = 9.0` at the 10% reference and `19.0` at the 5%.

**The weekday lock.** [proven] 14 days is two whole weeks; no phase drifts over 60 cycles.
Arithmetic, so structure-free.

**The scope limit, stated rather than buried.** "Both published structures" above means the
paper's model and both calibration branches of `PUB_B`. Under `PUB_A`, whose marker is a
state, the paper's quasi-steady model cannot represent the record at all: the RMS residual is
`2.0` to `2.6 sigma` on the recommended schedules and the reported `sd(ln theta)` is either
unavailable (a singular information matrix at the fit on every seed) or three to six times
worse (`0.69` and `1.05` against the paper's `0.19`). So the ranking is
not *demonstrated* under `PUB_A`; what is demonstrated there is better, and is the next
paragraph.

**Under `PUB_A` ONLY, the recommended schedules detect the wrong structure.** [measured] The
paper's fit leaves `2.0` to `2.6 sigma` of residual on 14.5 d, monthly and phase-spread, and
`0.0` to `0.6 sigma` on the two 14 d schedules, where it quietly returns `theta = 0` instead.
So against a turnover truth a phase-spread or off-period record is not only better
conditioned: it also tells the analyst the structure is wrong.

**Under `PUB_B` the fit is silent on EVERY schedule**, monthly and phase-spread included:
`0.000` to `0.19 sigma` of structural residual at the committed calibration, and `0.78` to
`0.88` with noise against an expected `0.816`. **So detection is a `PUB_A` property and not a
property of the recommended designs**, and the previous revision of this file said otherwise.
Against an algebraic-marker truth no schedule tested here raises a residual signal at all.

### Model-dependent: the value of `theta`, and everything stated in units of it

**`theta` is identified relative to a structure, not absolutely.** [proven] Under `PUB_B`,
`theta` enters `V = IC50 + theta` and `U`, and eliminating the other parameters leaves **no
relation on it**: any `theta` in `(0, V)` reproduces the record exactly. Walked explicitly,
`theta` runs from `3.06` to `3033` with the record unchanged to `1.4e-14`. Under `PUB_A` it
**is** identifiable, in closed form, `theta = (V-W)/Imax`. The difference is that `PUB_A`'s
marker is a state, which buys a number from the transient, and carries no free marker scale,
where `PUB_B` carries two and spends one.

**But `PUB_B` as published has no load input at all**, so where the load enters is our choice,
and the choice decides the answer. [measured] Of four plausible insertions, three leave
`theta` unidentifiable at any design and **one restores it**: the load lifting only the
suppressible pool `base`. **That this is the right reading is [judgement], not a finding.**
It rests on Attarwala's own definitions (`base` is "amenable to suppression", `E0` is "not
affected by mRNA-3927"), so a larger substrate load should raise the suppressible pool and not
the unaffected floor. Nothing in either source says where a load goes, and the rank result
above is what changes if the judgement is wrong.
Removing the free offset `E0`, or knowing `IC50` externally, also restores it. **So the honest
statement is that under `PUB_B` whether `theta` is identifiable at all turns on an assumption
the source does not make**, and the deficit traces specifically to carrying a free marker
offset alongside a free marker amplitude, which is what a phenomenological readout has and a
flux balance does not.

**So `theta_hat` can be badly wrong, sometimes with no warning in the fit.** [measured]
Fitting the paper's model to `PUB_B` records returns `theta_hat` high by `1.29` to `2.03` at
the committed dose and by `4.3` to `20.5` at a dose calibrated to the falls the trial actually
reports. **Three conditions on those figures**, all established in the insertion follow-up:

- **The direction is robust; the size is not.** `theta_hat/theta` is above 1 under every load
  insertion and every estimator tried, but it is `1.3` to `1.7` with the paper's own priors and
  `5` to `9` with them removed. **The priors shrink the bias, they do not prevent it.**
- **The absence of a warning is insertion-dependent.** The residual is `0.18` to `0.24 sigma`
  when the load scales the whole marker or the suppressible pool, and `0.93` to `1.27` when it
  moves the unsuppressible part, against an expected `0.816` for a well-specified fit.
- **So is identifiability itself.** See below.

**`K_m` moves with it.** [measured] `100` under the paper's model; `116` to `165` under
`PUB_B` at the committed calibration, `354` to `1086` at a 30% one, on the same schedules and
the same record quality.

**Every `sd(ln theta)` figure, including `0.1697`.** [measured] Conditional on the paper's
model, on its priors, and above all on the `k_e` prior, which does most of the identification.

**Every visit count.** [measured] `11` monthly visits to `sd(ln theta) = 0.20` on the
committed footing; `30` at a 7 d enzyme half-life; `42` or unreachable in five years if the
trial's reported fall is read at the dose peak; `10` to `12` if read at the trough. The
design's cost is not robust even within the paper's own model, because it depends on two
constants the sources do not pin.

**`+/- 36.9` and the second-order diagnostics.** [measured] Parametrisation-dependent, as
already recorded; right as computed in the paper's log coordinates.

**The three settling times.** [measured] Computed in the paper's model only, and not tested
against either published structure.

### The one thing that checks the model-dependent side

Nothing inside the longitudinal record can, because a well-fitting wrong structure is exactly
the case a residual cannot detect. The check has to be an **external anchor**: a measurement
taken outside the record. Tier 1 of the paper's specification is the only one it currently
has. That is drafted as passage 9 of `PHASE4_PROPOSED_TEXT.md`, with a pointer in the
discussion's "What would change the answer".

## 1. What is proven

**The constant-load degeneracy is an exact curve, not curvature.** [proven] Solving the
balance for the increment that produces a level `m` gives, with no remainder,
`Delta(m) = A/m + B + C m`, `A = K_m(L+b)`, `B = L+b-theta-k_e K_m`, `C = -k_e`. Three numbers
are all a constant-load record contains, so four unknowns leave a curve:
`K_m(b) = K_0 L/(L+b)`, `theta(b) = theta_0 + b + k_e(K_0-K_m(b))`, `k_e` fixed. Checked
symbolically, in exact rationals, and by the data-free identity
`residual_on_curve/residual_true = (K_0+m)/(K+m)`. A mutation-checked test is in the suite.

**The estimator returns that curve.** [proven] A variable-projection fit has one nonlinear
parameter; its objective is flat to `1.5e-19` across three decades of `K_m`, the fitted
parameters landing on the curve to `1.8e-8`.

**The linear reparametrisation is right, and is the PI's idea.** [proven] Multiplying by
`(K_m+m)` gives a form linear in `beta = (K_m, K_m b, b-theta-k_e K_m, -k_e)`. Symbolic
remainder zero.

**A visit's dose-cycle phase fixes its weekday, permanently.** [proven] 14 days is two whole
weeks. Checked against a calendar over 60 cycles: none of 56 half-day phases drifts.

**The capacity ceiling holds on both readings.** [proven] The used fraction is
`f(1+Delta_peak/theta)`, so `theta` cancels: exactly `47/150` at 10%, `47/300` at 5%.

## 3. What is conditional

**The diet record must carry real day-to-day variation.** [measured] It is the *recorded* `L_k`
that enters, so a record imputed from a prescription carries none. Without the load prior a
constant load is *exactly singular*, and a 2% recorded CV cannot reach the target in 60 visits.

**The priors do the identification, and `k_e` does most of it.** [measured] Monthly `n = 25`,
CV 10%, dose exact: paper's footing **0.1698**; load prior off **0.1723**; `k_e` flat
**3.9570**; both off **5.7432**. This *confirms* the stated footing rather than undermining it.

**The dose scale must be measured, and has not been.** [measured] The required CV, recomputed
over 8 diet records, is **0.1168** (0.1141-0.1187); printed 0.1167. At that CV monthly `n = 25`
reaches 0.20 **with no margin**, and Phase 3's 12-visit calendar needs **36 visits**. The
repository's own between-visit row is *mean 14.0%, range 0.4-50.8%*, an upper bound on what is
needed.

**The fortnightly result needs therapy to start at `t = 0`.** [measured] Already on therapy,
its `Delta/theta` range is **exactly zero** and it is exactly singular at a constant load.

**The weekday calendar needs exact 14-day dosing at a fixed hour.** [measured] Slips move
phases by up to a tenth of a cycle; precision barely follows, the map does not survive, so the
calendar must be re-derived from the actual infusion dates.

means, 0.1796 with the nuisances drawn from those priors, RMSE **0.1922** across 32 diet
records (0.1362-0.2557).

**`+/- 36.9` is right as computed.** [measured] My earlier contrary claim is **withdrawn**:

**The three settling times are all correct**, differing by operating point (4.2 vs 3.71 h) and

## 5a. Findings from the literature reviews. PENDING VERIFICATION, nothing applied

Supplied September 2026 from two literature reviews (clinical and methods). **No constant is
changed, no analysis is run on these, and no source is added to `paper/refs.sources`**; the
full reference list with DOIs is held in `refs/literature_pending.md`. Network egress is
blocked in this container, so **none of the citations below has been verified here**. Two of
the five are flagged against things this repository already holds, and one of those is a
direct conflict.

**1. PA diet records are prescriptions, not intake as eaten.** (Daly et al. 2017,
PMID 29021961; a 47-centre survey reporting prescribed protein.) No published within-patient
day-to-day variability of *actual* intake exists. **If this holds, it is the most consequential
item in either review**, because the `10%` recorded load CV is then unsupported and a
prescription-based record is effectively a **constant** recorded load. What that costs is
already measured and needs no new work: at a constant recorded load the paper's model is
**rank 3 of 4, exactly singular** (Phase 5 step 3b), and section 3 above already records that
a 2% recorded CV cannot reach the target in 60 visits. **So the question is no longer "how
much does the load vary" but "is the recorded load a measurement at all".**

**2. About half of propionate production is not dietary.** (Thompson et al. 1990,
*Metabolism* 39:1133; 6 patients, fasting, mixed PA/MMA: amino acids ~52%, gut bacteria
>=22%, odd-chain fatty acids ~30%.) Two consequences if it holds, both for `b`, the
unrecorded component of the load. **Its scale:** the prior is `S_B = 5%` of `L`, that is
`16.14` against `L = 322.73`, and `b_true = 0` in every simulation. A systematic
non-dietary half would put `b` at the order of `L` itself, roughly twenty prior standard
deviations out. **Its constancy:** the same review reports `b` moving with metronidazole
(about `-40%`), with illness and with fasting, whereas the model carries it as a single
constant. The mapping from "non-dietary propionate" to "`b`" is an interpretation and is
flagged as such.

**3. Residual PCC activity in PA is typically <5% of control.** (Hsia et al. 1979,
PMID 481943.) This bears directly on open question **(b)** above, which this audit
deliberately left open because `params.py` states the fraction two ways four lines apart. The
literature favours the **5%** reference. It changes no pass or fail (`h1_admissibility.py`
holds at both), and it changes the admissible range of the unidentified direction by `2.5x`.
**Not applied**: the repository's own rule is that the PI settles this, and the citation is
unverified.

**4. No human data exist on plasma 3-HP half-life, clearance or test-retest CV.** This
**confirms** rather than changes the repository's position: `params.py`'s `k_e` row already
calls it "the priority gap", and Phase 5 finding 2 removed the one extraction route the row
named, because the cited model's marker is algebraic and carries no elimination constant. The
review adds that absolute plasma 3-HP in `umol/L` is also unpublished for PA, both key human
papers reporting only percentage change or an undigitised figure. That is a third gap on the
same quantity.

**5. Breath-test repeatability. CONFLICT, and this repository has already adjudicated it.**
The review gives "CV 12.8% in MMA patients, 6.9% in controls (Manoli et al. 2021,
PMID 33820958)". That source is **already registered here** as `breath_test_mma`
(`doi:10.1038/s41436-021-01143-8`), and `REVIEW_LOG.md` **R4 retracted the 12.8% figure**:
Manoli's Results give `14.02 +/- 12.86%`, range `0.39` to `50.84`, and the abstract reports
the standard deviation as though it were the mean. R4 adds a second point the review does not
make, and it is the one that matters for Tier 1: the figure is **between-visit over two months
to four years**, with changing renal status named by the authors as a cause, and is therefore
an **upper bound** on the same-day repeatability Tier 1 actually needs. **The review's bullet
restates the abstract figure R4 corrected. Do not import it.** The review's other statement,
that no PA breath-test cohort exists, agrees with `08_specification.tex` as written.

## 5b. The open data questions

Three constants the paper needs and the primary sources do not pin. Each is now quantified,
and each was checked against the actual PDFs in Phase 5 rather than against a transcription.

**1. When was the trial's biomarker sample drawn?** The single highest-leverage gap. Extended
Data Table 3 of the phase 1/2 interim analysis reports 3-HP changes of `-29, -3, -20, +5,
-20%` "after the third dose" and **names no time point**. The Methods say only "from baseline
(predose levels) to post-dose levels". There is no biomarker time-course figure in the
article. The only sampling time named anywhere is a predose (trough) draw, in the antibody
paragraph.

> It matters this much: at the committed half-life, the trough reading needs `D = 12255` to
> `20977` and reaches `sd(ln theta) = 0.20` in **10 to 12** monthly visits; the peak reading
> needs `D = 766` to `1311` and reaches it in **42** visits or **not within five years**. One
> unstated sampling time moves the design from "better than we said" to "not feasible".

**The public record is exhausted.** The registry for `NCT04159103` has been checked: its 3-HP
time frame reads "Baseline (predose levels) to postdose levels ... up to Week 40", a window
and not a visit. So **the timing cannot be settled from public sources and must come from the
sponsor.**

**What the registry does establish** is that the sponsor holds full post-dose time courses: it
lists `Emax`, `AUEC` and duration of response among the biomarker outcomes, and none of those
can be computed from a single draw. **The economical ask is therefore `Emax` and `AUEC` per
cohort**, which would pin the dose calibration directly and make the sampling-time question
moot, rather than the raw per-visit series.

**2. How large is the dose effect?** `D_AMP = 6000` is calibrated to a `62` to `66%` marker
fall, against reported per-cohort falls of `-29%` to `+5%` with no dose trend. **The constant
is not moved**; the sensitivity table in `PHASE5_ROBUSTNESS.md` stands in its place, with
admissibility on every row. The per-cohort standard deviations, `24` to `57` percentage points
on cohorts of three or four, bound assay, within-patient and between-patient variability
jointly and separate none of them. A published dose-to-marker calibration in humans, or the
trial's own per-visit biomarker series, would close this.

**3. What is the enzyme half-life?** `lambda = ln 2/3.5 d` is annotated as corroborated by
`kdeg = 0.0075/h`. That is **one rate constant inside a two-compartment protein model** whose
own stated protein half-life is `~7 d`. The model's local half-life passes `3.5 d` at about
five days after a dose, reaches `9.0 d` by the end of a fortnightly interval, and tends to
`11.8 d`. So `lambda` tracks the published decline over the first week and decays faster
thereafter. **The constant is not moved**; passage 10b states the qualification. Moving it to
`ln 2/7` costs `11 -> 30` monthly visits and moves every Phase 3 figure.

**Two older ones, unchanged and still binding, and the first is now sharper.** The diet record
must carry real day-to-day variation, and no real diet record enters this audit anywhere.
Section 5a item 1 reports that PA records are typically prescriptions rather than intake as
eaten, which would make the recorded load constant and therefore exactly singular; that
citation is unverified. And the delivered dose must be
characterised to about `12%`, which is what Tier 1 asks for and what only a between-visit
upper bound is published for.

**One request outstanding:** Baek's Source Data file, which holds the mouse 3-HP time course
as numbers rather than as a figure. It would let the translational model be simulated end to
end instead of through a calibration of ours.

## 6. The one recommendation on uncertainty

**Report the profile interval with the `chi^2_1` threshold, and state its measured coverage
rather than calling it 95%.** [measured] Over 160 replicates per row (8 diet records x 20,
binomial se `+/- 0.02`) it covers **0.881** at CV 10% with the load prior off and **0.863**
with it on, against a nominal 0.95. It is never censored there and needs no bootstrap.

Four things beside it: [measured] conditional on the stated priors; **covers about 86 to 88%
at 10% recorded load variation, not 95%**; describes the prior's population, not one patient
(RMSE 0.093 at the prior means against 0.192 drawn); **void at a constant load without the load
prior**, where every method returns an unbounded interval while reporting 100% coverage.

**Read no coverage figure without its censoring column.** The profile's 0.975 at a 2% recorded
CV with the load prior off is not good coverage: about three-quarters of those intervals are
unbounded (78% on the main grid, 75% in the addendum's smaller run), so the hits are intervals
with no upper end. That row is a failed design, not a well-covered one.

**Do not use** the percentile bootstrap (0.77-0.82) or a parametric bootstrap at the fitted
nuisances (0.683). **Do not apply the boundary correction** [proven]: the 50:50
`chi^2_0`/`chi^2_1` mixture is for a parameter *being tested* on a boundary, and here that
parameter is a nuisance; applying it takes coverage from 0.938 to 0.887.

**On test inversion**, which the brief asked for and the first pass did not run: it covers
0.930-0.950 against the profile's 0.863-0.975, better and more consistent. **I still would not
report it** [judgement]: its intervals have no upper bound in 5-68% of cases, and its threshold
at the true `theta` is **0.26-0.43** of the empirical one in the textbook fixed-nuisance form,
so the coverage is not earned by calibration. Redrawing the nuisances inside it moves that
ratio to 1.11-1.77 and is the better variant. Use it to check the profile, not to replace it.

**If a stated 95% is required**, widen the threshold to the empirical LR 95% on the design in
hand: 4.1 to 6.9 here, not 3.84. [measured]

## 7. The design answer, in one line

**On the paper's own footing the monthly `n = 25` design reaches its 0.20 target for 59% of 32
SIMULATED diet records drawn at a 10% load CV** (median RMSE 0.1922, range 0.1362-0.2557).
[measured] At a 2% recorded CV without the load prior, **none of 32**.

**That 59% is conditional on the assumed 10% variation and is not a property of real
patients.** No real diet record enters anywhere in this audit; the load realisations are
lognormal draws at a CV the repository assumes. What the figure establishes is that even under
that assumption the design misses its own target for four records in ten, which is a weaker
claim than "it reaches 0.20" and a stronger one than any single median. The binding
requirements, in order: the `k_e` prior, real recorded diet variation, a measured dose scale.
