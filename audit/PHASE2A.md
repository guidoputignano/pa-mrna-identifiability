## A5 is the largest finding, and it was not the item expected to produce one

**The therapeutic increment is missing from the sensitivity matrix that computes the
paper's headline design numbers, and putting it back inverts the 1.8 gate.**

`src/common/excitation.py:sensitivities` builds the `ln K_m` column as
`-theta*Km*m/(Km+m)^2`. The model it is a linearisation of is
`dm/dt = L + L_0 - (theta + Delta(t)) g(m) - k_e m`, so the correct column is
`-(theta + Delta_i)*Km*m/(Km+m)^2`. The `ln theta` column is right as it stands
(`theta*g(m)`), because `theta` is the parameter and `Delta` is a known regressor.

`src/design/run.py:_filter` takes `levels` only. The levels were generated by
`quasi_steady_level(t)`, which *does* use `delta(t)` — `Delta/theta` runs 0.19 to 2.13
over the record. So the design is evaluated at levels the therapy produced, with a
sensitivity matrix that pretends the therapy is not there.

Reinstating it (`audit/a5_check.py`, agrees with the A5 verifier to four decimals):

| design | published | Delta reinstated |
|---|---|---|
| six-monthly clinic visits (n=4) | 0.5449 | 0.3507 |
| one dose cycle, every 2 days (n=7) | 0.5396 | **0.1511** |
| every 2 days for two years (n=365) | 0.4892 | **0.0251** |
| corr(ln theta, ln K_m), dense | +0.9988 | **-0.3163** |

The 1.8 gate is `sd(ln theta) < 0.20`. It **passes** on the dense design and passes on a
single seven-sample dose cycle. Section 7 currently reports "The gate fails, and the
failure is the result." The "factor of 122" from placement becomes 0.0251/0.00386 = **6.5**.

The mechanism is exactly Remark 11's: the ratio of the two forcings is
`-(1 + Delta/theta) * K_m/(K_m+m)`. Over the dose cycle `(1+Delta/theta)` varies by 2.6x
while `K_m/(K_m+m)` varies by 5%. **The excitation is bought by the dose train, not by the
marker band.** The paper's own therapy is the designed input it spent eight sections
saying did not exist.

### The obvious counter, and it bites hard

`Delta(t)` is only known if the product's PK is known. Treat the dose amplitude as a free
scale `kappa` (`audit/a5_kappa.py`), dense design, n=365:

| prior on the dose scale | sd(ln theta) |
|---|---|
| Delta known exactly | 0.0251 |
| 5% | 0.0519 |
| 10% | 0.0933 |
| 20% | 0.1744 |
| 30% | 0.2449 |
| flat | 0.5374 |

With `k_e` also fitted (A1's recommended footing): 0.1475 known, 0.2225 at a 20% prior,
0.5428 flat. **The gate passes only if the delivered enzymatic increment is known to
better than about 25%.** That is a PK/PD question about the product, not a sampling
question about the patient — and it is a question the trial can answer, which is more
than can be said for a 20-fold decompensation.

This supersedes A1's framing. A1's bridge (0.00386 -> 0.72 across estimator assumptions)
stands, but its 0.4892 baseline is the number this corrects.

## A1 — the 0.004 and the 6% never described the same object

Not a placement story. On one fixed design (straddling K_m, n=365) `sd(ln theta_hat)` is
0.00386 under 1.8's assumptions, 0.138 if `k_e` is also fitted, 0.027 if `k_e` stays known
but 1.9's heteroscedastic noise is used, and 0.72 under 1.9's actual estimator — a factor
of **185 from estimator assumptions alone**, before any difference in design.

1.8's own covariance propagated to the decision gives a **0.000% sign error for every
design in the paper**, the dense one included. 1.8's machinery cannot produce a nonzero
sign error anywhere, so no placement reconciles the two numbers. Section 7 runs two
mutually inconsistent estimators and quotes both as facts about the same record.

**The plateau attribution is refuted.** With the load bias still free and flat but the
noise made homoscedastic at 1.8's sigma, the sign error runs 32.3/26.7/2.1/0/0/0/0/0%.
With 1.9's noise and a feasible IRLS weighting, 35.6/26.9/13.0/3.0/1.0/0.6/0.2/0.2%,
monotone. The 6% is a property of `fit_ensemble`'s unweighted least squares against
level-dependent noise (per-sample sd runs 21.96 to 945.6 across the band), **not** of the
load record and **not** of Proposition 2(2).

**The curve does not plateau.** Extended: 35.2/28.9/17.0/7.5/5.5/6.5/10.7/16.3% — a
minimum near 40x, then rising. Cramer-Rao `sd(mu)` with the load bias free falls
monotonically (862 -> 428 over 20x to 320x), collinearity weakening 0.981 -> 0.781. So
"which excursion does not touch" is false as printed.

On A1's recommended footing the placement advantage is **10.74x**, not 122x.
`tests/test_models.py:1205` (`assert s_dense/s_placed > 50`) fails there.

## A2 — GATE: PASSES. The fibre is a property of the parent. The stated reason is wrong.

At matched parameter count the parent has an exact one-parameter degenerate family,
admissible over K_m 50..800 at chi2 <= 0.0035, against Model A's own fibre at <= 0.0088 —
**the parent's fibre is if anything harder to reject**. No escalation.

The paper's mechanism is false. `recover_from_ab`'s docstring and `03_dynamic.tex:301-306`
say the parent has no fibre because alpha is curved. But Model A with `k_e` **known**
inverts its own tangent by the identical formula to K_m = 100.00000000000000 at relative
error 0.0 — better than the parent's own inversion. What closes the fibre is the
alternative channel being **known**, in either model; curvature changes the numbers, not
the dimension count. This is the same mechanism as A1's and A5's.

Do not transport the fibre's closed forms. The parent's soft direction has
`d ln theta/d ln K_m = -0.154` against the local `+1.099` — opposite sign, 56.3 degrees
apart. The local fibre curve anchored on the parent's actual tangent is rejected at
chi2 = 1324 at its own base point.

Two reversals worth keeping: with the load-bias column in, the parent is **3.26x worse**
than the local model (325.56 vs 99.92), so "most favourable structure" does not hold in
that respect. And the null-genotype calibration does **not** close the fibre
(alt_peak 147..581, always below 600) but does kill the low-theta tail: theta >= 72..122
rather than down to 1e-3.

`margin_window`'s two-endpoint premise is structurally invalid in the parent — theta is
non-monotone along the fibre, interior minimum at K_m = 122.

## A3 — No. The 20x excursion is not observed, and neither is the 8x the code hardcodes.

No propionate marker has a documented excursion to 20x, or to 8x, above a patient's own
managed level. The only quantified decompensation-vs-baseline comparison for the chosen
marker is Haijes 2020 Table 4 — a table titled *"diagnostic biomarkers for PA and MMA
that are not significantly altered during AMD"*: urinary 3-HP median 232 [89-349] (N=10)
outside crisis against 738 [184-1534] (N=13) during, **Bonferroni P = NS**, unpaired,
urine not the plasma the specification names. Ratio of medians **3.18x**. Plasma C3 in the
same table *falls* (0.91x).

Two source errors to fix: the plasma methylcitrate No-AMD cell is empty — 49.5 umol/L is
the **crisis** value and must never be quoted as a baseline. And the natural history
study's "up to three times higher" is a between-participant contrast cited to Ryu 2012,
which contains no quantitative 3-HP value at all. The citation chain is broken at the
primary source.

"Twenty times" carries no empirical content of its own: it is arithmetic on
`m_bar/K_m = 0.1`, both ends marked ASSUMED in `tab_params`. The only empirical claim in
those sentences is the appositive "which is a severe decompensation", which has no `\cite`
anywhere and no `claims.csv` row.

At 3.18x the repo's own code returns E = 0.150 (30% of the 0.50 required) and a sign error
of **~30%**, not 10%. The ranking survives — a decompensation still beats the best routine
source at E = 0.062. The closeness does not: "within a factor of two of the requirement"
becomes a factor of **3.3 short**.

Marker-specific, not metabolic: ~20x AMD excursions *are* documented in urinary lactate
(19.1x, P < 0.0001) and 3-hydroxybutyrate (36.6x, P = 0.0052), same design.

Internal inconsistency nobody closed: the illness amplitude `excursion_for_decision`
itself fixes (4.07x load) places the marker at 5.36 m_bar, `fig_decision` sweeps only to
8.30 m_bar, and `tab_params`' own illness amplitude gives 1.57 m_bar. The 20 m_bar row is
asked of a patient the paper simultaneously places at 5.4 m_bar.

## A4 — No, and this one has to be stated, not scoped away

The single-state model does not hold on the band the specification recommends, and the
failure is not the one the paper flags.

**Where the equilibria are.** The parent's clearance peaks at 3.36 m_bar and its
separatrix sits at 12.61 m_bar. So 41.7% of the log-band [1, 8] m_bar lies beyond the peak
where the equilibrium is *unstable*, and the specification's 14.6 and 20 m_bar rows lie
past the separatrix where **no equilibrium exists at all**. Both estimators are
steady-state fits to (marker, load) balance pairs. Over most of the excitation-bearing
band there is no steady state to fit. (Reproduced: `audit/a4_check.py`.)

**The two models disagree by 4.6x about the largest clearable load.** Model A needs a
steady load of 5.37x baseline to hold 8 m_bar; the parent's clearance ceiling is 1.177x
baseline, full stop. ParamsB makes it three-way: the same non-enzymatic channel is 4.5% of
baseline clearance in Model B, 15.5% in A, 77.5% in C.

**The adiabatic elimination is invalid at this scale.** The right test is the full
system's slow eigenvalue against the reduced model's own relaxation rate: 0.4733 vs
2.0200 per day at the module's default k, so the reduction is **4.3x too fast**, and is
within 10% only for k >= ~30 (a carnitine pool turning over in under an hour). Probe 1's
"time-scale separation 9.8" is real but points the wrong way — the gap is 75.8 exactly
where the reduction is worst, because at low k the *eliminated* variable is the slow one.

**The sharpest number.** Fit the design's own admissible form with `k_e >= 0` to the
parent's **noiseless** clearance on 9 levels: theta_hat = 557.8 at 1.6 m_bar, 277.0 at 5,
**151.01 at 8**, 27.3 at 12.6, 0.00 at 20. Against theta_crit_intrinsic = 150.775:
structural misfit alone, with no noise and no perturbation to the patient, consumes 99.8%
of a true margin of +149.2 and puts mu_hat at +0.24. By 12.6 m_bar it flips the sign
outright. Beyond 5 m_bar the misfit *is* visible in the residuals, which closes off the
"merely noisy" defence.

**Direction of failure reverses.** `fit_ensemble`'s ordinate is the recorded load. Crisis
protocol stops natural protein while catabolic muscle breakdown supplies the load, so
recorded intake falls towards zero while m climbs — least squares must drive theta_hat
towards zero. That reverses the paper's signature 6%/53% asymmetry from "fails towards
declaring patients safe" to "declares every patient unsafe".

**Housekeeping that matters:** `rem:scope` is a duplicate label (`main.log:972`), so every
`\ref{rem:scope}` in the body resolves to the appendix copy on p.49, which is worded more
strongly than the one the body means. `rem:scope` is cited zero times in Sections 5-8 —
the sections that rest entirely on decompensation data.

## What this does to Phase 2B as written

2B's corrected target claim rests on "1.9 shows the sign error plateauing because of load
confounding, which excursion cannot reach. That makes load-record precision the decisive
design variable." **A1 refutes the premise**: the plateau is an artefact of unweighted
least squares against level-dependent noise, and Cramer-Rao sd(mu) falls monotonically in
excursion. **A5 relocates the decisive variable again**: it is the precision of the
delivered enzymatic increment, not the load record and not the excursion.

2B.5 asks for the data specification to be rewritten as a request for historical
decompensation admission records. A4 says the model the specification is derived from does
not hold there, and the parent says most of that band has no steady state to fit.
