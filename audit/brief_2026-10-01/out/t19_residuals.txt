================================================================================================
  ITEM 10.  RESIDUAL DIAGNOSTICS: IS THE MM SPECIFICATION RIGHT?
================================================================================================

  CAVEAT FIRST: all data is simulated from the one-compartment MM model,
  so MM residuals are white by construction. This is a POWER analysis of
  the diagnostic, not a test of the model. A real test needs real data.

  (0) SANITY: residuals under the TRUE MM model are structureless.
      monthly record, test rejection rate at 5% (should be ~5%):
        vs marker 0.0%, vs time 7.0%, vs load 4.0%

  (1) SATURABLE NON-ENZYMATIC CLEARANCE (k_e m -> Vne m/(Kne+m)).
      Kne = 150 (smaller = more saturable):
        monthly:   power vs marker 0%, vs time 2%, vs load 2%  (mean residual-vs-marker t = -0.35)
        excursion: power vs marker 0%, vs time 100%, vs load 100%
      Kne = 60 (smaller = more saturable):
        monthly:   power vs marker 0%, vs time 6%, vs load 3%  (mean residual-vs-marker t = -0.44)
        excursion: power vs marker 0%, vs time 100%, vs load 100%

  (2) LOAD MISMATCH: a 10%/20% linear drift of true load off its record,
      beyond the constant bias b the model allows.
      drift 10%: monthly power vs marker 0%, vs time 88%, vs load 4%
      drift 20%: monthly power vs marker 0%, vs time 100%, vs load 8%

  (3) SECOND COMPARTMENT: a slow exchange pool. At STEADY STATE the
      input-output balance is unchanged, so the monthly record is BLIND
      to it; it only shows in a transient as a second relaxation phase.
      exchange rate kx = 0.5/d: peak |d ln m| gap single vs two-pool = 5% of the decay rate (0% at steady state; grows in the transient)
      exchange rate kx = 2.0/d: peak |d ln m| gap single vs two-pool = 15% of the decay rate (0% at steady state; grows in the transient)

  VERDICT (power of residual diagnostics, by mechanism and design):
   - The residual-vs-MARKER test is degenerate here: the MM fit is itself
     a marker model, so it removes marker-linear structure by construction
     (0% even under a real misspecification). The vs-TIME and vs-LOAD
     tests carry the power.
   - SECOND COMPARTMENT: the monthly record is BLIND (identical steady-state
     balance); it shows only in a transient's second decay phase.
   - SATURABLE non-enzymatic clearance: the monthly record is nearly BLIND
     (narrow on-therapy m-range looks linear; power ~5% at n=25). A dense
     load EXCURSION detects it with ~100% power -- the same richer data
     that identifies theta.
   - LOAD MISMATCH (a time-varying drift): the monthly record CAN catch it,
     via residual-vs-time (88-100% power at a 10-20% drift). The constant
     bias b absorbs only a constant offset, not a trend.
   - So the load-record assumption is partly checkable now; a second
     compartment and saturable clearance are not, short of a transient or
     excursion. And with NO real data, this remains a power analysis, not
     a test of the specification.
