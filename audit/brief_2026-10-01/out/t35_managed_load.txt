==============================================================================================
  ROUND 8 ITEM 2. theta_crit MOVES WITH THE MANAGED LOAD; the margin mu
==============================================================================================

  For a literature-consistent LOW ceiling (take ke=100, well below the untreated
  load L0=276, so theta=0 is NOT maintainable -- the severe phenotype):
  theta_crit = L - ke, and the 5% patient (theta=150) is stable iff
  L < theta + ke = 250. The margin is mu = theta - theta_crit(L).

        managed load L   reduction vs L0  theta_crit (%norm)    mu (%norm)   5% stable?
                   276              0.0%                5.9%        -0.87%  NO (diverges)
                   260              5.8%                5.3%        -0.33%  NO (diverges)
                   250              9.4%                5.0%         0.00%  NO (diverges)
                   240             13.0%                4.7%         0.33%          yes
                   220             20.3%                4.0%         1.00%          yes
                   200             27.5%                3.3%         1.67%          yes
                   180             34.8%                2.7%         2.33%          yes

  READING: untreated (L=276) the 5% patient is BELOW theta_crit and diverges.
  A load reduction of ~9% (to L<250) brings them above it; deeper
  management buys margin. Protein restriction + gut suppression routinely cut the
  load 20-40% (Thompson: gut share ~22%, ~41% suppressible; protein restriction
  cuts the ~52% amino-acid share), so a stable managed steady state at 5% is
  ACHIEVABLE -- which is why real patients are stable. The correct statement is:
  the severe patient has a steady state only UNDER active load management, with a
  thin margin mu that the record's own antibiotic/protein excursions (paper 3.5)
  can erase -- a theta_crit excursion is a decompensation.
