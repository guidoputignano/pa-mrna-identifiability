============================================================================================
  ITEM 2. THE CROSS-SECTIONAL CONCLUSION IS MODEL-DEPENDENT
============================================================================================

  Stated per model (cross-sectional sd(ln theta) at 5% of normal, monthly record):
    Model A (the paper's working model):            3.05   -> no feasible route
                                                            (misses 0.20 by ~15x)
    Model B, literature-consistent, managed load:   0.29   -> still misses 0.20,
                                                            but by ~1.5x, not ~15x
  Model A is the model the parent paper ITSELF calls inadequate (its linear
  clearance predicts no decompensation; the paper keeps A only as the most
  favourable *identifiability* case, Remark 15). So the headline 'no marker-only
  design reaches target cross-sectionally' is a Model-A statement; under the more
  faithful B the gap is a factor of ~1.5, not ~15.

  PRIOR SENSITIVITY (the improvement is bought with prior information, not data):
    Model B, managed, ke prior 0.8, Ke pinned:   0.29
    Model B, managed, ke prior 0.8, Ke free 0.5: 0.32
    Model B, managed, NO priors, Ke free:        52.82   <- exchange degeneracy
    Model A, NO ke prior:                        5.86
    So B's 0.29 rests on the k_e/K_e priors; strip them and B is far WORSE
    than A (the a-fortiori of Remark 15). The gain is prior-bought, not data-bought.

  WHAT WOULD REACH 0.20 UNDER B? (tighten the nuisance priors at the valid point)
      k_e prior sd   Ke pinned   Ke prior 0.3   Ke prior 0.1
              0.80      0.292          0.301          0.293 
              0.40      0.217          0.229          0.219 
              0.20      0.194*         0.207          0.195*
              0.10      0.187*         0.201          0.189*
              0.05      0.186*         0.200*         0.187*
    (* = reaches 0.20.) Reading: under B, a k_e prior tightened from 0.8 to
    ~0.2 (with K_e known) reaches target. A sd(ln k_e)=0.2 k_e prior is COMFORTABLY
    MET by the IV 13C3 tracer (5.4% = sd 0.054, R-G; tighter than the 0.2 needed,
    so at the tracer's own precision B reaches ~0.19) or the safe transient at L0
    to 1% (R-B) -- so UNDER MODEL B the cross-sectional target is reachable with a
    k_e sensor the brief already prices, a far cheaper requirement than the
    direct-enzyme calibration Model A forces. This is model-dependent and worth
    stating as such: the whole 'only a direct calibrated enzyme read works'
    conclusion is partly an artefact of Model A's low enzyme share.

  WHAT WAS CHOSEN, AND ON WHAT BASIS (nothing here is fitted to a patient):
    - ceiling k_e = 100: chosen BELOW the untreated load (~276) so theta=0 is not
      maintainable (the severe phenotype); literature-consistent (non-PCC disposal
      is low-capacity) but not a measured value -- the repo gives no k_e in units.
    - managed load L = 220: chosen to ADMIT a steady state at 5% with a ~1%-of-
      normal margin (a ~20% reduction from the untreated anchor). Also a choice.
  Because both are choices, the honest output is a RANGE over their plausible
  spans, not the single 0.29.

  RECONCILING THE LOAD-REDUCTION FIGURE WITH THE PAPER'S OWN NUMBERS:
    - This script's margin needs a ~10-20% load reduction to lift a 5% patient
      above theta_crit (t35).
    - The LITERATURE (Thompson 1990 et al., round-6 research; not the parent
      paper's own text) puts management's load reduction at 20-40% (standard of
      care: protein restriction + gut suppression), and the parent paper states
      that the SMALLEST illness
      excursion that strands a patient is +18% in the load (s_fold 706 vs
      baseline 600 at theta=300, sections/03_dynamic.tex).
    - These are the SAME ORDER: the reduction management must supply (~10-20%),
      the reduction it does supply (20-40%), and the excursion that strands
      (18%) all sit in a ~10-40% band. So the patient lives in a narrow margin --
      normal management keeps them just inside it and a single ~18% illness
      pushes them out. The margin mu and the trigger amplitude are comparable,
      which is why decompensation is a recurring event rather than a rare one.
