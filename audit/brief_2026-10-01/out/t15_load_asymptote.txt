================================================================================================
  ITEM 13.  THE LOAD-RECORD TERM: BIAS vs SCATTER, AND THE n -> inf LIMIT (k_e EXACT)
================================================================================================

  (1) DECOMPOSITION OF THE 0.377 AT n = 25 (k_e exact, K_m free):
      full (5% assay, 5% load scatter, 5% bias prior):  0.377
      remove the per-draw load SCATTER (LOAD_FRAC->0):   0.188   (scatter worth +0.188)
      remove the systematic BIAS b (measure L exactly):  0.283   (bias worth    +0.094)
      remove BOTH load terms (assay + K_m only):         0.188
      -> at n = 25 the per-draw SCATTER costs more than the BIAS.
      -> the user's premise (bias is the binding term) does not hold here.

  (2) n -> inf WITH k_e EXACT. Does sd asymptote above 0.20?
             n   b 5% prior  b pinned (L exact)    sd*sqrt(n), b 5%
            25       0.3766              0.2827               1.883
           100       0.2725              0.1441               2.725
           365       0.1905              0.0740               3.639
           730       0.1496              0.0524               4.042
          1460       0.1120              0.0371               4.278
          5000       0.0629              0.0200               4.449
         20000       0.0321              0.0100               4.543
        100000       0.0144              0.0045               4.562
      sd*sqrt(n) settles to a constant: the fall is ~1/sqrt(n), limit 0.
      NEITHER the bias nor the scatter creates a positive floor: both are
      learned from the dose-train modulation of the operating point m.
      => 0.151 at 730 draws is NOT flattening toward a floor. Item 2 is
         not answered by an asymptote; it is answered by feasibility (item 12).

         X% on L  n=25, k_e exact  n=25, k_e to 0.2  n=365, k_e exact

  (5) WHERE L BINDS, AND WHAT MEASURING IT TO ~1-2% BUYS (three places):
      (i) the 0.39 partition  : 0.38 -> 0.30 at n=25 (this script, part 4).
      (ii) the washout intercept L0 (t7): the single biggest rung on the
           knowledge ladder -- withdraw-half goes 9.4 -> 1.5 -> 0.63 as L0
           is pinned to 5% then 1% (t7_design.txt). But the washout route
           is ruled out on safety (item 15), so this lever is unavailable.
      (iii) the flux route (item 11): the flux reading needs ABSOLUTE
           calibration; measuring L to X% is one way to supply it via the
           (s, L) pair. See t16.
