# Quantitative Evidence on Dietary Protein Intake Variability and Plasma Biomarkers in Propionic Acidemia and Analogue Protein-Restricted IEMs

## TL;DR
- **Direct PA data are essentially absent for your core question:** there are NO published within-person day-to-day coefficient-of-variation (CV) figures for protein or amino-acid intake in propionic acidemia (PA), and NO study that directly regresses visit-to-visit or day-to-day dietary intake against a PA plasma biomarker (ammonia, propionylcarnitine/C3, methylcitrate, glycine). You must borrow from phenylketonuria (PKU, the best analogue) and general nutrition-epidemiology literature, and flag these as analogues.
- The strongest quantitative anchors are: (a) in PKU, actual natural-protein intake runs ~167% above prescription (median +40%, p<0.001; n=16 children); (b) in well-controlled PKU, incidental extra Phe intake of 100% (sometimes 200%) of usual daily intake produces NO significant blood-Phe rise (van Rijn 2011, n=6), while only large sustained pharmacologic loads move blood Phe (+~603 µmol/L, n=28); and (c) in healthy adults, estimating an individual's usual protein intake to ±10% requires far more days than energy (energy 27–35 days; protein up to 151 days).
- For your model, treat dietary protein as a high-variance, noisy input (protein has among the highest within:between-person variance ratios of any macronutrient, ratio >1) whose short-term coupling to plasma markers is heavily buffered — a single day's intake is a poor predictor of a single plasma value, and the relationship is better modeled as damped/threshold with a lag than as a linear slope.

## Key Findings
- **Section 1 (intake variability):** No PA-, MMA-, UCD-, or MSUD-specific within-person CV of protein/amino-acid intake exists (explicit data gap). In healthy populations, protein's within-person variance meets or exceeds between-person variance (variance ratio >1) and needs many days of recording per individual (energy 27–35 days; protein up to 151 days for ±10%).
- **Section 2 (recording/adherence):** Clinics prescribe protein but measure actual intake poorly; prescribed and actual diverge substantially (PKU actual natural protein ~167% of prescribed). Adherence (measured by % of blood values in target) declines steadily with age, from ~89% under 2 years to ~40% over 41 years.
- **Section 3 (digital tools):** A small set of IEM-specific tools exist (PKU Bite RCT, Metabolic Diet App Suite, mFood photography app for UCD); feasibility/efficacy data exist but biomarker-validated accuracy data are limited to a single UCD study.
- **Section 4 (intake↔biomarker):** In well-controlled PKU the incidental-intake→blood-Phe relationship is weak/buffered; large sustained loads are the exception that raises blood Phe substantially.
- **Section 5 (PA specifics):** Limited "as-eaten" data exist only as small retrospective case series; during decompensation protein is stopped entirely for ~24–48 h then reintroduced.

## Details

### Section 1: Within-person day-to-day variability (CV) of protein and amino-acid intake

**(a) PKU patients — ANALOGUE.** No study reports a formal within-person CV of daily protein or Phe intake in PKU. The clearest quantitative evidence of day-to-day intake variability is the UK Phe-exchange study (Evans/MacDonald group, "Natural Protein Intake in Children with Phenylketonuria: Prescription vs. Actual Intakes," *Nutrients* 2023;15(23):4903, DOI 10.3390/nu15234903, PMC10708375; n=16 children, median age 11 y, 14/16 classical PKU; prospective, 12–18 one-day diaries + ~22 blood spots per child over 6 months). Value: actual natural-protein intake was **167% higher than prescribed**; median prescribed natural protein 6 g/day vs actual 10 g/day; median prescribed Phe 300 vs actual 500 mg/day; median prescribed-vs-actual difference **+4 g/day natural protein (range −2.5 to +11.5)**, a **median +40% increase (p<0.001)** — explicitly described as producing "a variable daily protein intake," yet 91% of blood Phe values stayed within target range (Results/Abstract).

**(b) MMA, UCD, MSUD — ANALOGUE; explicit data gap for within-person CV.** No within-person day-to-day CV of intake is published for any of these disorders. Only between-person SDs from cross-sectional cohorts exist:
- MMA: patients tolerated 99.45 ± 32.05% of the RDA for complete protein (mut0 subgroup) — Manoli et al., "A critical reappraisal of dietary practices in methylmalonic acidemia…Part 1," *Genet Med* 2016 (PMC4752925; n=61 isolated MMA; cross-sectional natural-history study). This is a between-person SD, not a within-person CV.
- Adult UCD/branched-chain organic acidemia: mean total protein intake 0.61 ± 0.2 g/kg/day and mean natural protein 0.54 ± 0.2 g/kg/day (7-day food-diary study, n=18 adults; *Nutrients*/PMC8839969).
- MSUD: dietary leucine tolerance is defined per-individual (mg/kg/day) but no day-to-day CV is published (GeneReviews, NBK1319).

**(c) Healthy children and adults (nutrition epidemiology) — ANALOGUE.**
- *Within- vs between-person variance:* protein intake generally shows within-person variance ≥ between-person variance (variance ratio >1). In a Japanese national dataset (Fujiwara et al., *J Nutr* 2026, PMID 41997489; nonconsecutive 8-day records, n=1,375 M / 1,422 F), within:between variance ratios ranged 0.71 (potassium) to 3.08 (n-3 fatty acids). In US children/adolescents ages 6–17 (NHANES 2007–2010, n=3,473; Jahns et al., *J Acad Nutr Diet* 2015, S2212267214003591), variance ratios exceeded 1 for all nutrients and were "generally higher for protein."
- *Days needed for usual individual intake:* Basiotis et al. (*J Nutr* 1987;117:1638–1641; n=29 adults, USDA Beltsville year-long weighed records) found that to reach within 10% of true intake, energy required on average **27 days (men, n=13) and 35 days (women, n=16)**, whereas protein required more; downstream reviews cite **31–64 days** for macronutrients at 95% confidence. Rossato & Fuchs (*Front Nutr* 2021;8:667031, DOI 10.3389/fnut.2021.667031; population 48-h recall, n=237) reported that to capture 10% of within-person variation, protein needed **151 days** — far more than energy (7 days) or carbohydrate (18 days) (Results, Table 4). Beaton's classic work (cited in the NCBI DRI bookshelf, NBK217540) noted an intraindividual CV for energy of ~25% of the mean, requiring ~3 weeks of data for ±10% energy confidence limits.
- *Ranking precision:* to rank-order individuals at r=0.8, NHANES children needed 6–9 recalls and adolescents 3–6 (Jahns et al. 2015).
- *Digital-cohort estimate:* the Food & You study (MyFoodRepo AI app, n=958, 315,000 meals; PMC12537485) found protein reached r≈0.8 reliability within 2–3 days — lower than weighed-record estimates, attributed to lower reporting bias with app methods.

Interpretation: protein is one of the noisiest macronutrients to pin down at the individual level; any PA model should assume a large day-to-day within-person CV and cannot treat a 1–3 day record as "usual" intake.

### Section 2: How protein intake is recorded/monitored in metabolic clinics

- **Prescribed vs actual (agreement):** In PKU, prescriptions systematically underestimate actual intake — actual natural protein 167% of prescribed, actual Phe median 500 vs prescribed 300 mg/day (PMC10708375). In UCD, "assessment of actual protein intake is challenging as standard assessment methods are recognized to be inaccurate, time-consuming, and cumbersome" (Mol Genet Metab 2025, PMID 41242089). This is the clearest published prescribed↔actual agreement datum; no formal correlation coefficient between prescribed and consumed protein is published for any IEM (data gap).
- **Methods used:** weighed food records, 3-day diet records, 24-h recalls, Phe/protein exchange systems, semi-quantitative FFQs (a validated low-protein FFQ exists for PKU, PMC9031454), and increasingly food-photography apps. Clinical-trial protocols (e.g., the Kuvan pediatric trial, NCT00838435) mandate a 3-day dietary record before each clinic visit reviewed by a study dietitian.
- **Frequency of assessment:** disease- and age-specific. MSUD infants are monitored weekly or twice-weekly and older children/adults every 1–2 weeks (GeneReviews NBK1319; StatPearls NBK557773). PA/MMA guidelines call for routine ongoing monitoring and titration to biochemical parameters (PA nutrition guideline, Jurecki et al./Mol Genet Metab 2019, S1096719218303421).
- **Adherence rates (quantitative):**
  - The general benchmark comes from WHO's 2003 report *Adherence to Long-Term Therapies: Evidence for Action* (ISBN 92 4 154599 2): "Adherence to long-term therapy for chronic illnesses in developed countries averages 50%. In developing countries, the rates are even lower." This ~50% figure is widely applied to PKU.
  - Adherence in PKU (indexed by % of blood Phe values in target) declines with age. A European cohort (2012–2018, n=1,323; PMC11243388) reported the proportion of blood-Phe values within target as: <2 years 89%; 2–5 years 84%; 6–12 years 73%; 13–18 years 85%; 19–30 years 64%; 31–40 years 59%; and ≥41 years 40%.
  - UK adult PKU cohort (PMC6835765): non-adherent patients derived only 3.9 ± 9.2 g/day protein from substitutes vs 58.6 ± 10.2 g/day in adherent patients (q<0.001), with blood Phe 861 ± 348 vs 464 ± 196 µmol/L (q=0.040).
  - Greek cross-sectional cohort (PMC12608463; n=27): 66.7% complied with the PKU diet and recommended protein substitutes.
  - UCD/PA: protein aversion drives poor compliance and risk of chronic protein deficiency (28-patient UCD retrospective review, PMID 24857408). **All Section-2 figures are analogue (PKU/UCD/MMA); none are PA-specific "as-eaten" adherence statistics.**

### Section 3: Digital/app-based dietary/protein tracking tools in IEM

- **PKU Bite (RCT) — ANALOGUE (PKU).** Evans et al., "Efficacy of a New Low-Protein Multimedia Diet App for PKU," *Nutrients* 2022;14(11):2182 (DOI 10.3390/nu14112182, PMC9182776, PMID 35683982). 12-week RCT, 60 caregivers of children aged 1–16 y + 21 adolescents; app (>1,100 foods, colour-coded, protein calculator) vs written/pictorial material. Results: no difference in label-reading knowledge or self-efficacy; a *trend* to improved protein-calculation accuracy with the app (baseline→12 weeks: app 35%→48%; control 39%→35%). App popularity 43% (equal to contacting the dietitian, 43%). Described as the first PKU diet app studied systematically and validated by healthcare professionals. No biomarker-accuracy validation.
- **Metabolic Diet App Suite — ANALOGUE (PKU/MSUD/others).** Ho et al., Mol Genet Metab 2015 (S1096719215300913). Covers PKU, MSUD, glutaric acidemia, PDE; estimates amino-acid content as a fixed % of protein when foods lack specific data (Phe 5%, leucine 6% for MSUD, lysine 4%). Positive user feedback; feasibility only, no biomarker validation.
- **mFood food-photography app (UCD) — ANALOGUE (UCD); the only IEM app validated against a biomarker gold-standard.** "Measuring dietary intake among participants with a urea cycle disorder using standard diet records or a novel food photography app," *Mol Genet Metab* 2025;146(4):109291 (DOI 10.1016/j.ymgme.2025.109291, PMID 41242089). Randomized crossover, n=8 adults ≥16 y with UCD; mFood vs traditional 3-day diet records, both validated against doubly-labeled-water total energy expenditure (TEE). Result: no statistical difference in grams of macronutrients between methods; protein was 13% of energy (diet records) vs 15% (mFood); vs TEE, participants under-reported total energy by 16% (diet records) and 22% (mFood) — i.e., mFood was NOT superior to and slightly worse than traditional records for accuracy, but was preferred by most participants.
- **Point-of-care Phe monitoring (adjacent, biomarker not intake):** the phenyx app + PHE sensor at-home self-testing system (medRxiv 2025, feasibility NCT05998109) and the Egoo Phe Test (Lin's concordance ρc = 0.91 vs dried blood spots; PMC12512701, n=20 females). These monitor the biomarker, not intake, but are relevant to your modeling of biomarker sampling error.

### Section 4: Studies directly relating day-to-day/visit-to-visit intake variation to a plasma biomarker (PKU as analogue)

The dominant, well-replicated finding is that in *well-controlled* PKU on a stable diet, incidental dietary Phe increases produce little or no measurable blood-Phe rise (buffered by protein synthesis and high day-to-day biological variability); a clean published Pearson/Spearman r or a µmol/L-per-mg regression slope for natural-Phe intake vs blood Phe does not appear to exist. Instead, the relationship is characterized by load/challenge and threshold designs. **All are PKU analogues.**

- **van Rijn et al., "Adult patients with well-controlled phenylketonuria tolerate incidental additional intake of phenylalanine," *Ann Nutr Metab* 2011;58(2):94–100 (DOI 10.1159/000324924, PMID 21474926).** Longitudinal; n=6 well-controlled adults; encapsulated L-Phe loads of 100% and 200% of each patient's individual daily Phe intake, once per week. Pre-load vs post-load mean blood Phe not significantly different at 100% load; the 200% load produced only a larger variation. This is the direct basis for the widely quoted statement (as restated in Rocha et al., Nutrients 2019, PMC6566391): "Van Rijn M. et al. has shown in six well-controlled patients that an incidental additional Phe intake of 100% and even 200% extra for one week each was tolerated without affecting blood Phe control." Reported day-to-day blood Phe varied 55–257 µmol/L; individual SDs 6–99 µmol/L.
- **van Spronsen et al., *Pediatrics* 1996;97:839–844 (PMID 8657524).** n=7, ages 1–20 y; a single meal delivering 75% of the daily Phe allowance raised mean plasma Phe only +13%, transient (return to baseline within 2–3 h).
- **Rohde et al., *Eur J Clin Nutr* 2012;66:633–638 (PMID 22318648).** 4-week randomized crossover, n=14 children 2–10 y; free fruit/vegetable consumption increased total Phe intake by a mean of +58 mg/day (p=0.037) but dried-blood Phe was unchanged.
- **Daly et al., "Impact of Fruit and Vegetable Protein vs. Milk Protein…," *Nutrients* 2021 (PMC9611310).** 5-phase crossover, n=16 children (median 10.5 y, 14/16 classical PKU); stepping intake +50→+100 mg/day Phe moved median blood Phe 240→260→280 µmol/L. Threshold effect: all tolerated +50 mg/day from fruit/veg, but only 11/16 (69%) tolerated +100 mg/day from fruit/veg, and only 8/16 (50%) tolerated +50 mg/day and 5/16 (31%) +100 mg/day from milk — i.e., source-dependent threshold, not a linear slope.
- **Pinto/Rocha et al., *Nutrients* 2019;11(5):995 (PMC6566391).** n=40, mean age 17 y (12–29 y), mixed severity; verbatim: "Twenty-six patients (65%) were able to increase their median NP intake by a median 12 g/day (2–42 g)/day… median blood Phe levels remained unchanged (279 vs. 288 μmol/L, p = 0.06)." Effect size: +12 g/day natural protein → +9 µmol/L blood Phe, non-significant.
- **MacLeod et al., *Mol Genet Metab* 2009;98:331–337 (PMID 19747868).** n=8 adults; 7/8 increased Phe tolerance by 15–173% without significantly increasing blood Phe.
- **PICO study, *JIMD*/PMC11558469 (PMID 39544686).** Randomized placebo-controlled crossover, n=28 adults; sustained high Phe capsules over 4 weeks — verbatim: "The median Phe level increased from 852 μmol/L (interquartile range: 345) to 1455 μmol/L (interquartile range: 369)" (+~603 µmol/L). This is the exception showing large *sustained* loads do raise blood Phe substantially.
- **Body-composition study (Robertson et al., Mol Genet Metab Rep, S2214426920300458).** n=30 pediatric PKU; synthetic (substitute) protein intake (g/kg) negatively correlated with dried-blood-spot Phe (p=.04); natural protein intake NOT related to blood-spot Phe; age (p=.02) and fat-mass% (p=.02) positively correlated.
- **Fluctuation magnitude (context):** across the review literature (Cleary et al., Mol Genet Metab 2013, S1096719213003119), median plasma-Phe daily variation was 155 µmol/L (range 80–280) in 16 classic PKU patients aged 1–18 y — driven mainly by protein-substitute timing rather than by Phe intake. MacDonald's group also found a strong negative correlation between protein-substitute taken by the evening meal and the daytime fall in plasma Phe.
- **No comparable intake↔biomarker regression exists for PA plasma markers (ammonia, C3 propionylcarnitine, methylcitrate, glycine, or the C3/C2 ratio) — explicit data gap.**

### Section 5: PA-specific data

- **"As-eaten" intake — DIRECT PA (small retrospective series):**
  - Sequeira et al., "Long term follow-up of the dietary intake in propionic acidemia," *Mol Genet Metab Rep* 2021 (PMC8082257, PMID 33981581): retrospective long-term follow-up of 4 PA patients; total protein intake above the RDA with high dietary Leu/Val and Leu/Ile ratios; cohort averaged 3.16 ± 0.86 unwell episodes/year and 2.06 ± 0.69 hospital admissions/year for metabolic decompensation.
  - Natural-history study (PMC8411103; n=4 children): energy 80–120% of DRI, intact protein below current guidelines, total protein adequate via medical formula, but persistently reduced height Z-scores.
  - Dutch retrospective series (n=17; van der Meer et al., Eur J Pediatr, DOI BF01953939): mean natural protein 6.3 ± 1.5 g/day (early-onset) vs 17.6 ± 5.3 g/day (late-onset).
  - European dietary-practice survey — Daly et al., "Dietary practices in propionic acidemia: A European survey," *Mol Genet Metab Rep* 2017 (PMID 29021961, PMC5633157; 186 PA patients, 47 centres, 14 countries): verbatim — "Seventy-four per cent of patients were prescribed natural protein intakes below WHO/FAO/UNU (2007) safe levels… PFAA were used to supplement natural protein intake in 81% (n = 38) of centres, providing a median of 44% (14–83%)… Sixty-three per cent (n = 117) of patients were tube fed (74% gastrostomy)." Note: these are prescription/cross-sectional data, not day-to-day "as-eaten" logs. **No study reports repeated daily "as-eaten" intake logs paired with same-day PA biomarkers — explicit data gap.**
- **Intake/protein tolerance during acute illness/decompensation — DIRECT PA (protocols and case reports):**
  - PA acute protocols (New England Consortium of Metabolic Programs; BIMDG "Propionic Acidaemia — Acute Decompensation" protocol): "All protein intake should be halted once it is determined that a patient with PA is having metabolic decompensation severe enough to need treatment and acute metabolic management," with high-dextrose (10% glucose) IV fluids at 1.5× maintenance and gradual reintroduction of protein once stable (typically over ~24–48 h).
  - PA nutrition guideline (Jurecki et al., Mol Genet Metab 2019, S1096719218303421): protein advanced with intact protein by 0.25–0.5 g/kg/day as tolerated; TPN during acute crises minimizes catabolism.
  - Case report (PMC5911373): a frequently-decompensating PA infant maintained ~0.8 g/kg/day protein between episodes (0.2 g/kg/day as PFAA), titrated to 1.2 g/kg/day by last assessment.
  - Analogue acute protocols: MMA — catabolic stress raises the offending amino acids (isoleucine, valine, methionine, threonine) and propionic/methylmalonic acid (New England Consortium MMA protocol). MSUD sick-day protocol — 120% increase in BCAA-free formula, 50–100% decrease in leucine intake, fluids 150 mL/kg/day (StatPearls NBK557773).

## Recommendations
1. **Model dietary protein as a high-variance, noisy input.** Because no IEM-specific within-person CV exists, borrow a within-person CV in the ~25–40% range (from healthy-population energy/protein figures — energy intraindividual CV ~25%, protein variance ratios >1) and label this an analogue assumption. Benchmark to revise: if a PA cohort ever publishes paired daily records, replace this with the observed CV.
2. **Do not assume a single day's intake predicts a single plasma value.** The PKU evidence shows strong buffering — incidental changes of 100–200% of daily Phe produce no significant blood change, while only large sustained loads (weeks) move the biomarker. Build a damped/threshold response with a time lag rather than a linear slope. Change threshold: if you obtain PA-specific paired intake–C3/methylcitrate data with r>0.5, shift toward tighter coupling, especially during catabolic states.
3. **Require multiple days for "usual intake."** Adopt ≥3 days minimum for ranking and recognize that ±10% individual precision for protein realistically requires weeks. If an app supplies the data, expect ~16–22% energy under-reporting (mFood/UCD benchmark) and adjust accordingly.
4. **Model decompensation as a regime switch, not a continuous function.** During acute illness, PA protein intake drops to zero for ~24–48 h regardless of the prescribed diet, and the biomarker is driven by endogenous catabolism, not intake — a distinct state your model should treat separately from steady-state.
5. **Prioritize obtaining primary PA paired data.** The single largest gap is day-level intake + same-day PA plasma biomarker. Approach investigators of the NIH/consortium PA natural-history cohorts (Manoli/Venditti and the European E-IMD registry) for de-identified individual-level longitudinal data; this is the only way to replace analogue assumptions with PA-specific effect sizes.

## Caveats
- Nearly every quantitative intake↔biomarker effect size here is a PKU analogue. PA biochemistry (ammonia, propionylcarnitine C3, methylcitrate, glycine) may couple to intake differently — and plausibly more tightly during catabolic stress — than blood Phe does in PKU. Do not assume the PKU "buffering" generalizes to PA decompensation.
- Core PKU loading/challenge studies have very small samples (n=6–40), limiting precision of the effect sizes.
- No published within-person day-to-day CV of protein or amino-acid intake exists for ANY protein-restricted IEM (PA, MMA, UCD, MSUD, PKU). The macronutrient-variability figures are from general nutrition epidemiology.
- App accuracy validation against an objective biomarker (doubly-labeled water) is limited to a single 8-patient UCD study; PKU app data (PKU Bite) measure calculation accuracy and behavior, not biomarker agreement.
- PA "as-eaten" data are confined to small retrospective case series and cross-sectional prescription surveys; there are no prospective repeated-daily-record datasets in PA.
- Where sources conflict on days-needed for protein (e.g., Basiotis' 27–35 days for energy vs Rossato's 151 days for protein), the difference reflects method (weighed records vs 48-h recall) and nutrient (energy vs protein); protein consistently needs many more days than energy, which is the robust conclusion.
