# Systematic Evidence Review: How Quickly Plasma Organic Acid Markers Respond to Changes in Metabolic Capacity or Load in Humans — Testing the Assumption That Plasma 3-Hydroxypropionate (3-HP) in Propionic Acidemia Reaches a New Steady State Within Hours

## TL;DR
- **The core assumption — that plasma 3-HP in propionic acidemia (PA) reaches a new steady state within hours — is only partially supported and is NOT directly evidenced.** Substrate/*load*-driven changes (protein load, fasting, catabolism) shift propionate-pathway markers within hours, but *capacity*-driven changes (liver transplant, gene/mRNA therapy) take days-to-a-week, and no published human study reports a formal plasma 3-HP half-life, clearance, or steady-state equilibration time.
- **The evidence base is highly asymmetric.** The most quantitative kinetic data are preclinical (mouse mRNA/gene therapy: biomarker nadir ~24 h); data are moderate for liver transplantation (days-to-week to a new but still-abnormal steady state) and dialysis (rapid fall with well-documented rebound); and essentially absent for formal human pharmacokinetics and for biological variability of 3-HP and 2-methylcitrate.
- **Best defensible working model:** 3-HP most plausibly behaves like a rapidly-turned-over small hydroxy-acid analogous to methylmalonic acid (MMA) and propionate — equilibrating over hours-to-~1 day for a pure load change, but reaching a new steady state over *days* when the change is in metabolic capacity, because extrahepatic (gut, muscle, kidney) production continues to supply the pool.

## Key Findings

1. **No direct 3-HP kinetic data exist.** There is no published human plasma terminal half-life, clearance, or renal fractional-excretion value for 3-HP that can be cited. All inference must come from analogues (MMA, propionate, 2-methylcitrate, propionylcarnitine/C3) and from indirect decay/rebound curves after transplant and dialysis.

2. **Propionate itself is cleared very rapidly** (minutes-scale, dominated by near-complete hepatic first-pass extraction in normal physiology), making it the fastest-responding analogue. This supports rapid *load*-responsiveness but says little about how fast the pool re-equilibrates when endogenous production continues.

3. **Liver transplantation produces a steep fall over hours-to-days to a new, still-abnormal steady state within roughly the first post-operative week — not within hours.** 2-methylcitrate (in PA) and MMA (in MMA disease) characteristically remain elevated because of continued extrahepatic production; combined liver-kidney transplant lowers MMA more than liver alone.

4. **Dialysis/hemofiltration lowers ammonia and organic acids rapidly but with well-documented rebound** after intermittent hemodialysis (HD) is stopped — the central rationale for preferring continuous renal replacement therapy (CVVHDF/CRRT). Most *quantitative* extracorporeal clearance data are for ammonia, not the organic-acid markers themselves.

5. **Pharmacologic/dietary interventions act on distinct timescales:** protein load and fasting shift markers within *hours*; metronidazole (suppression of gut-flora propionate production) acts over *days*; carglumic acid lowers ammonia within *hours* but does not directly lower the organic-acid markers; L-carnitine shifts acylcarnitine partitioning (raising measured C3) over hours-to-days.

6. **Biological-variability data are a genuine gap.** MMA biological variation has been characterized in general-population biomarker studies (EuBIVAS/Carobene), but there is **no dedicated within-subject biological-variation study for plasma 2-methylcitrate or 3-HP in stable PA/MMA outpatients.**

7. **The most quantitative kinetic marker data are preclinical.** Moderna's dual-mRNA program for PA (mRNA-3927; Jiang L et al., *Nature Communications* 2020;11:5339) and its mRNA program for MMA (An D et al., *Cell Reports Medicine* 2019) show rapid biomarker reduction (nadir within ~24 h) with duration of suppression on the order of 1–2 weeks per dose in mouse models. AAV gene therapy (Chandler RJ & Venditti CP; Guenzel/Barry) produces slower-onset but far more durable (months) suppression.

## Details by Requested Category

### Category 1 — Plasma half-life, clearance, and renal excretion
This is the **weakest-evidenced category**; there are essentially no rigorous formal pharmacokinetic (t½, CL, FE%) studies of these specific analytes in humans.

- **3-HP:** No published human half-life, clearance, or renal fractional-excretion value. **[GAP — NO RELIABLE DATA].**
- **2-methylcitrate (2-MC):** No formal human PK value; behaves as a tricarboxylic acid and is renally excreted. **[GAP].**
- **Propionate:** In normal human physiology, short-chain fatty acids including propionate are cleared very rapidly by near-complete hepatic first-pass extraction (turnover on the order of minutes), established in colonic-infusion and stable-isotope SCFA turnover physiology. No clean PA-specific plasma half-life exists. **[CONFIRMED-CONCEPT for the minutes-scale hepatic clearance; NEEDS PRIMARY VERIFICATION for any specific PA number].**
- **Propionylcarnitine (C3):** Governed by whole-body carnitine turnover and renal excretion; no isolated terminal half-life value known. **[GAP / NEEDS PRIMARY VERIFICATION].**
- **MMA:** Freely filtered with substantial renal clearance (plasma MMA falls sharply after kidney/liver-kidney transplant and during dialysis, indicating a high renal contribution), but no formal terminal half-life value can be reliably cited. **[CONFIRMED-CONCEPT for renal handling; NEEDS PRIMARY VERIFICATION for any t½].**

**Bottom line for Category 1:** State explicitly in the review that formal PK data (t½, CL, FE%) for 3-HP, 2-MC, C3, and MMA in humans are essentially absent, and that clearance behavior is inferred *indirectly* from (a) post-transplant decay curves (Category 2) and (b) dialysis rebound curves (Category 3). Recommended verification searches: *methylmalonic acid renal clearance fractional excretion*; Manoli/Sloan/Venditti MMA renal handling; Wolfe/Bergman colonic-propionate turnover.

### Category 2 — Time course after liver / combined liver-kidney transplantation
This is the **strongest human dataset** for "how fast markers change" following a change in metabolic *capacity*.

- **Critelli K, McKiernan P, Vockley J, et al., "Liver Transplantation for Propionic Acidemia and Methylmalonic Acidemia: Perioperative Management and Clinical Outcomes," *Liver Transplantation* 2018.** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] Reports peri-operative metabolic marker behavior in PA/MMA recipients; markers (C3 and 2-MC in PA; MMA in MMA disease) fall substantially within days but do not normalize. *[Exact per-timepoint values and n NEED PRIMARY VERIFICATION.]*
- **Vara R, et al., liver transplantation for PA in children, *Liver Transplantation*.** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] Documents reduced metabolic crises and biochemical improvement post-LT; C3/organic acids improve but methylcitrate remains persistently elevated. *[Values/n NEED VERIFICATION.]*
- **Sloan JL / Manoli I / Chandler RJ / Venditti CP (NIH MMA natural-history & transplant cohort; *Molecular Genetics and Metabolism* / *JIMD*).** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] After liver or combined liver-kidney transplant in *mut* MMA, plasma MMA typically drops by roughly an order of magnitude (~80–90% reduction) but remains well above normal because MMA is produced in extrahepatic tissues; the fall occurs over days. *[Exact percentages and time points NEED VERIFICATION.]*
- **Molema F, Williams M, et al. (Rotterdam); Kasahara M et al. (Japanese pediatric LT for organic acidemia); Curnock R et al. (pediatric transplant series).** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] Report outcomes and biochemical improvement after LT/combined transplant.
- **Berry GT et al.** — metabolic physiology of transplant in organic acidemia; the whole-body-vs-hepatic-correction concept (markers only partially corrected because extrahepatic propionate/MMA production continues). [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT.]

**Robust conclusion you can state:** After liver (or combined liver-kidney) transplantation, PA/MMA plasma markers fall steeply over the first hours-to-days and reach a new, *lower-but-still-abnormal* steady state within roughly the first post-operative week; residual elevation reflects continued extrahepatic production. This is a **days-scale, not hours-scale, equilibration** for a capacity change.

### Category 3 — Time course during/after haemodialysis or haemofiltration (acute decompensation)
- **Baumgartner MR, Hörster F, Dionisi-Vici C, et al., "Proposed guidelines for the diagnosis and management of methylmalonic and propionic acidemia," *Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases* 2014.** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] Key citable source discussing dialysis indications and rebound qualitatively.
- Hemodialysis (HD) has higher instantaneous small-solute clearance than CVVH/CVVHDF; ammonia typically falls >50% within a few hours. A **well-recognized rebound** occurs after intermittent HD is stopped, because of ongoing endogenous production and redistribution from tissue compartments — hence the frequent preference for continuous therapy (CVVHDF/CRRT) to prevent rebound. [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT.]
- **Quantitative dialysis clearance (mL/min) for the individual organic-acid markers** (MMA, propionate, 2-MC, 3-HP) is largely **absent**; most quantitative extracorporeal data in this space are for *ammonia*. **[GAP for organic-acid-specific clearance.]** Suggested verification: Picca S / Schaefer F on extracorporeal detoxification in inborn errors; *CVVHDF methylmalonic acidemia neonate*.

**Interpretation:** Dialysis demonstrates that these pools *can* be drawn down within hours, but the documented post-cessation rebound is direct evidence that the pool does **not** stay at a new low steady state on an hours timescale once the external clearance is removed — production refills it. This argues against a simple "within hours to new steady state" assumption whenever endogenous production is intact.

### Category 4 — Time course after starting/stopping carnitine, metronidazole, carglumic acid, or a protein load
- **Metronidazole:** Bain MD, Borriello SP, Tracey BM, Jones M, Reed PJ, Chalmers RA, Murphy GM (and Thompson GN & Chalmers RA) [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT]. Metronidazole reduces propionate production by suppressing anaerobic gut flora that generate propionate from undigested protein/fiber. The effect on urinary propionate-derived metabolites develops over **days** of dosing (reflecting gradual gut-flora suppression), with large reported reductions in urinary propionate metabolites. *[Exact % reduction/n NEED VERIFICATION.]*
- **Carglumic acid / N-carbamylglutamate:** Valayannopoulos V et al.; Levrat V et al. [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT]. In PA/MMA hyperammonemic crises the effect is on **ammonia** (activates CPS1/N-acetylglutamate synthase pathway), with ammonia lowering within **hours**. It does **not** directly lower the organic-acid markers (2-MC, MMA, 3-HP). Important nuance for the review: carglumic acid is an *ammonia* story, not an *organic-acid-marker* story. *[Values NEED VERIFICATION.]*
- **L-carnitine:** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] Facilitates propionyl removal as propionylcarnitine, thereby *raising* measured plasma/urine C3 while conjugating the accumulated propionyl moiety; acylcarnitine profile shifts occur over hours-to-days. No precise verified kinetic value. *[NEEDS VERIFICATION.]*
- **Protein load test:** Thompson GN, Chalmers RA, Walter JH [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT]. Historic work showed organic-acid marker excretion rises within **hours** of a protein/amino-acid load (and during fasting/catabolism), demonstrating rapid substrate-driven responsiveness of propionate-pathway metabolites. *[Exact time-to-peak and magnitude NEED VERIFICATION.]*

**Robust conclusion:** Gut-flora-mediated interventions (metronidazole) act over **days**; substrate/catabolic drivers (protein load, fasting) shift markers within **hours**; carglumic acid changes ammonia (hours) but not the organic-acid markers directly; carnitine shifts acylcarnitine partitioning over hours-to-days. This split is the crux of the answer to the central question: the *load* axis is fast, the *capacity/production* axis is slow.

### Category 5 — Visit-to-visit and within-day variability (CV%) and analytical CV
- **MMA biological variation:** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] MMA is among analytes characterized in the European Biological Variation Study (**EuBIVAS; Carobene A et al.**, *Clinical Chemistry* / *Clin Chem Lab Med*) and in the B12-deficiency biomarker literature. Reported within-subject biological variation (CVi) for serum/plasma MMA is relatively modest (broadly in the low-to-mid teens percent range). Critically, these are for **general-population MMA (B12 status context), NOT the vastly elevated disease-range MMA of MMA disease.** *[Exact CVi/CVg/CVa and reference-change value NEED PRIMARY VERIFICATION — cite EuBIVAS/Carobene directly.]*
- **Analytical CV of LC-MS/MS and GC-MS for MMA:** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] Validated clinical assays generally report intra- and inter-assay CVs in the single-digit-to-low-teens percent range (e.g., Blom HJ; Windelberg; Schmedes method-comparison work). *[Exact CVa NEEDS VERIFICATION.]*
- **2-methylcitrate, 3-HP, and C3 visit-to-visit variability in stable PA/MMA outpatients:** **No dedicated biological-variation study is known.** This is a **[GENUINE GAP — NO RELIABLE PUBLISHED DATA]** and should be stated explicitly. C3 analytical CV is available from newborn-screening MS/MS QC literature (CLSI/CDC NSQAP), but *biological* visit-to-visit CV in PA patients is not characterized.

**Interpretation:** Without characterized within-subject variability for 3-HP and 2-MC, it is impossible to define a reference-change value (RCV) — meaning that even if a marker "moved," we currently cannot say whether an hours-scale change exceeds analytical + biological noise. This is a foundational gap for any claim about how fast 3-HP reaches a "new" steady state.

### Category 6 — Animal (mouse) and clinical mRNA/gene-therapy time courses (NON-HUMAN unless stated)
This is where the most **quantitative** kinetic marker data actually exist.

- **Jiang L, Park J-S, Yin L, et al., "Dual mRNA therapy restores metabolic function in long-term studies in a mouse model of propionic acidemia," *Nature Communications* 2020;11:5339** (Moderna mRNA-3927 program; co-delivered LNP-formulated mRNAs encoding human PCCA and PCCB). **[NON-HUMAN — mouse.]** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] A single IV dose in PA (Pcca-hypomorphic) mice produced rapid reduction of plasma **2-methylcitrate** and **methylmalonic acid** (used as PA biomarkers), with maximal suppression within **~24 h** and duration of suppression on the order of **~1–2 weeks per dose**, supporting repeat dosing. *[Exact % reduction, time-to-nadir, per-dose durability, and n NEED PRIMARY VERIFICATION from the figures.]*
- **An D, Schneller JL, Frassetto A, et al., "Systemic messenger RNA therapy as a treatment for methylmalonic acidemia," *Cell Reports Medicine* 2019 (and earlier *Cell Reports* 2017)** (Moderna mRNA-3704 / hMUT mRNA-LNP). **[NON-HUMAN — mouse.]** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] In *Mut*-deficient MMA mouse models, IV LNP-hMUT mRNA rapidly lowered plasma **MMA** (large reduction, on the order of ~75–90% within **~24 h**), with the effect waning over roughly **1–2 weeks**, supporting repeat dosing. *[Exact numbers, doses, time-to-nadir, duration, and n NEED VERIFICATION.]*
- **Chandler RJ & Venditti CP — AAV gene therapy for MMA and PA (NIH). [NON-HUMAN — mouse.]** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] AAV8/AAV9-MUT in *Mut-/-* mice produced **durable (months)** reduction in plasma MMA; onset over **days-to-weeks**, durability far longer than mRNA, reflecting persistent transgene expression. *[Values/n NEED VERIFICATION.]*
- **Guenzel AJ, Barry MA et al. — AAV gene therapy PA/MMA. [NON-HUMAN — mouse.]** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT] Demonstrated marker reduction and survival benefit in murine models. *[Values NEED VERIFICATION.]*
- **Human clinical trials (Moderna):** Paramount trial of **mRNA-3927 (PA)** and Landmark trial of **mRNA-3705 (MMA)** — interim data reporting reductions in metabolic decompensation events and biomarker (2-MC, MMA) signals with repeat IV dosing have been presented, but largely in **conference abstracts / press releases** rather than peer-reviewed primary papers. **Any specific human biomarker-kinetics number in this space must be verified against the actual abstract/publication before citation.** [CONFIRMED-CONCEPT that trials and interim biomarker reporting exist; specific numbers NEED VERIFICATION.]

**Interpretation:** The murine ~24 h time-to-nadir after a bolus of restored enzymatic capacity is the closest existing kinetic analogue for "how fast a capacity change moves the markers." It sits between "hours" and "days" — supporting a **~1-day** rather than a **few-hours** equilibration for 3-HP after a capacity change, and cautioning against the "within hours" assumption. Note the important interspecies caveat: mouse turnover is generally faster than human, so the human timescale is, if anything, likely to be *longer*.

## Directly Answering the Central Assumption
**Does plasma 3-HP in PA reach a new steady state within hours after a change in metabolic capacity or load?**

- **For a pure *load* change** (e.g., protein load, fasting, a meal, an acute catabolic stress): **Plausibly yes, hours-scale** — supported by (a) protein-load studies showing organic-acid excretion rising within hours (Thompson/Chalmers), and (b) the minutes-scale hepatic clearance of propionate. The upstream substrate flux changes fast, and the markers track it.
- **For a *capacity* change** (transplant, enzyme restoration via mRNA/gene therapy, dialysis, drug that alters production): **Likely NO — the timescale is days, not hours.** Supported by (a) transplant decay curves reaching a new abnormal steady state over ~a week, (b) murine mRNA nadir at ~24 h (and human turnover likely slower), and (c) dialysis rebound demonstrating the pool refills from ongoing extrahepatic production once external clearance stops.
- **For 3-HP *specifically*: there are no direct human data at all.** The assumption is an extrapolation. Given 3-HP's chemistry (small, water-soluble, renally excreted, generated continuously from accumulated propionyl-CoA), it most likely equilibrates faster than 2-MC but on a similar order to MMA — i.e., hours-to-~1-day for load, days for capacity.

**Verdict:** The blanket assumption of "within hours" is **defensible only for load-driven changes** and is **not supported for capacity-driven changes**, where the honest estimate is **days**. Any model or study design relying on the "within hours" premise for a capacity change should be revised or explicitly flagged as an untested assumption.

## Recommendations (staged, with decision thresholds)
1. **Do not assume an hours-scale steady state for 3-HP after a capacity change.** For modeling transplant, mRNA, or gene-therapy effects, adopt a **days-scale equilibration** (anchored to transplant decay curves and the ~24 h murine nadir), not hours. Reserve the hours-scale assumption for pure load manipulations.
2. **Match sampling design to the intervention's mechanism.** For a protein-load or fasting study, dense sampling over the first 2–8 h is appropriate. For a capacity intervention (drug that alters production, transplant, gene/mRNA therapy), sample over days-to-weeks with at least daily early points.
3. **Close the two highest-value gaps first:** (a) a formal **biological-variation study of plasma 3-HP and 2-MC in stable PA outpatients** to establish analytical + within-subject CV and a reference-change value (without this, "new steady state" is undefined); and (b) **dense post-dose 3-HP time-course sampling in the Moderna mRNA-3927 (Paramount) PA trial**, which would provide the first direct human capacity-change kinetics for these markers.
4. **Before publishing any number in this review, re-extract exact values from the named primary sources** — prioritizing, in order: Baumgartner 2014 (*Orphanet J Rare Dis*), Critelli 2018 (*Liver Transplantation*), Jiang 2020 (*Nat Commun* 11:5339), An 2019 (*Cell Rep Med*), the Sloan/Manoli/Venditti MMA cohort papers, the Bain/Chalmers metronidazole papers, and Carobene/EuBIVAS for MMA biological variation.

**Benchmarks that would change these recommendations:**
- A human study demonstrating a 3-HP nadir and re-equilibration **<6 h** after an acute *capacity* change would validate the "within hours" assumption and warrant tighter early sampling.
- Conversely, confirmation of transplant/mRNA rebound or re-equilibration over **>48 h** would strengthen the days-scale recommendation and argue for longer follow-up windows.
- Publication of a within-subject CV for 3-HP >20% would materially widen the reference-change value and require larger effect sizes to claim a "real" change.

## Caveats
- **Live search/PubMed retrieval was non-functional in this environment.** This review reflects established literature knowledge with explicit confidence labels; it is **not** a substitute for verified extraction from primary sources, which must precede publication. No DOIs, sample sizes, or decimal values were invented.
- **Direct 3-HP data are absent throughout.** Every quantitative statement about 3-HP is an extrapolation from analogues (propionate, MMA, 2-MC, C3), each clearly labeled.
- **Preclinical (mouse) kinetics may not translate to humans**; rodent metabolic turnover is generally faster, so human timescales are likely equal or longer.
- **"New steady state" is operationally undefined** for 3-HP and 2-MC because their biological/analytical variability has never been characterized in this population.
- Several key human datapoints (Moderna trial biomarker kinetics) currently reside in **conference abstracts/press releases**, which are lower-tier evidence than peer-reviewed primary papers and may be subject to revision.
