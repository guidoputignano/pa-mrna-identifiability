# Repeatability of 13C Breath Tests and Whether a 1-13C-Propionate Breath Test Can Detect a ~12% Change in Liver Enzyme Activity

## TL;DR
- A ~12% change in enzyme activity is detectable with the 1-13C-propionate breath test at the group level or with replicate testing, but not reliably from a single before/after pair in one person. The only published propionate repeatability data (Manoli 2021) give a within-subject CV of about 6.9% in healthy controls and about 12.8-14% in MMA patients over intervals of 1-3 weeks to years, which implies a smallest detectable individual change of roughly 19% from a single test pair.
- There are NO published same-day (within-day) propionate breath test repeatability data. The shortest documented repeat interval is 1-3 days (Wagner 2011 B12 breath test) and 1-3 weeks (Manoli 2021 healthy controls). Everything else is days to years.
- Comparator liver-function 13C breath tests cluster at a within-subject CV of about 10-15% for their best (cumulative recovery) metric (methacetin AUC0-60 CVp 10%; aminopyrine cPDR ~15%; KICA ~12%), so the propionate test's ~7-13% is at the favorable end. Its precision is adequate for detecting a ~12% change only with adequate replication or sample size.

## Key Findings

**1. The propionate test has good but not same-day-validated repeatability.** The single modern characterization (Manoli et al. 2021, NIH/NHGRI) reports a mean within-subject CV of 6.94% in healthy adults (7 subjects, 3 repeats each, 1-3 week intervals) and 14.02% in mut MMA patients (15 subjects, months to years apart). No study has performed two propionate tests on the same day.

**2. A 12% change is at the edge of the test's single-measurement resolution.** Using the healthy-control within-subject CV of ~6.9%, the repeatability coefficient (smallest change detectable in one person from a single pre/post pair) is about 19%. A 12% change therefore requires either replicate tests per timepoint or a group of subjects.

**3. Comparator tests behave similarly**, with cumulative recovery (area under the curve) being the most reproducible metric and peak height and time-to-peak metrics substantially worse.

**4. Breath tests have tracked real enzyme-activity change after intervention.** Propionate oxidation fully normalized after liver transplant in MMA; the erythromycin breath test rose 86 ± 30% after rifampicin induction; the 13C-phenylalanine test rose after sapropterin in PKU responders.

## Details

### Topic 1: 1-13C-propionate breath test, all published uses and repeatability

**Manoli et al. 2021, Genetics in Medicine 23:1522-1533. DOI 10.1038/s41436-021-01143-8; PMID 33820958; PMC8354855.** (Correction: DOI 10.1038/s41436-021-01188-9; PMID 33972722.)
- Population/design: 57 MMA subjects (38 mut0, 7 mut-, 10 cblA, 2 cblB; 19 transplant recipients) plus 16 healthy volunteers (8 healthy adults, 8 heterozygote parents). 83 propionate breath tests total. Natural history protocol NCT00078078. Dose 0.5 mg/kg sodium 1-13C-propionate oral or G-tube; VCO2 measured by indirect calorimetry (metabolic cart); 2-hour breath collection; IRMS (Metabolic Solutions) plus BreathID Exalenz. Minimum 3-hour fast (12 h if tolerated). (Methods)
- Repeatability, controls: "seven healthy volunteers repeated the study three times, with 1- to 3-week intervals between repeat testing... the coefficient of variation (CV) for dose metabolized within each subject ranged from 1.83% to 13.33%, with an average of 6.94 ± 5.3%, while the CVs between the seven subjects was 11.9%, 9.6%, and 5.6% for each of the three sets of replicate studies." (Results)
- Repeatability, MMA: "the CV for 1-13C-propionate dose metabolized in 15 mut patients, who had repeated breath testing 2-3 times each, ranged from 0.39% to 50.84% with an average of 14.02 ± 12.86%" at intervals "ranging from 2 months to 4 years." (Results)
- ABSTRACT vs RESULTS DISCREPANCY (flagged): The Abstract states "mean coefficient of variation 6.9% and 12.8%, respectively" for controls and MMA subjects, but the Results section gives the MMA average as 14.02 ± 12.86%. The control figure agrees (6.9% abstract vs 6.94% results); the MMA figure does not (12.8% abstract vs 14.02% results). Use the Results figure.
- Method comparison: IRMS vs BreathID r = 0.996 (P<0.0001), Bland-Altman 95% limits of agreement -2.386% to +3.266% (9 MMA subjects). (Results)
- Serum MMA CV in the same repeat-tested patients ranged 1.5% to 75.3%, average 31.38 ± 24.55%, i.e., much more variable than the breath test. (Results)
- Group values at 120 min (cumulative dose oxidized): controls 47.96 ± 4.26%; heterozygotes 41.6 ± 5.05%; mut0 21.77 ± 9.50%; mut- 42.88 ± 9.02%; cblA 47.75 ± 5.91%; cblB 31.78 ± 21.82%. (Fig 2 / Results)
- Therapy tracking: complete restoration of propionate oxidation to control levels after liver or combined liver/kidney transplant (3 LT/LKT tested pre/post), smaller change after isolated kidney transplant (3 KT); a cblA patient improved 2 months after resuming B12 injections, with serum MMA falling from 68 to 14.84 µmol/L.
- No same-day repeats.

**Barshop et al. 1991, Pediatric Research 30:15-22. DOI 10.1203/00006450-199107000-00004; PMID 1909779.** Original 1-13C-propionate method; IV or oral bolus (100 µmol / 9.7 mg/kg). Population: 5 PA, 8 MMA (4 B12-responsive), 1 multiple carboxylase deficiency, 1 transcobalamin-II deficiency, 5 controls. 3-hour breath sampling; cumulative recovery from predicted resting energy expenditure. Recovery of 13CO2 in PA significantly less than normal; MMA below normal but greater than PA. Therapy signal: in a B12-responsive MMA patient "no differences were noted within 2 d of initiating treatment with B12, but there was an increase in 13CO2 production after 4 mo"; only one PA patient showed increased 13CO2 with biotin. No repeatability CV reported.

**Wagner et al. 2011, Journal of Breath Research 5:046001. DOI 10.1088/1752-7155/5/4/046001; PMID 21697586.** B12 breath test (BBT) using sodium 1-13C-propionate. This is the closest thing to short-interval propionate repeatability data.
- Design: 58 subjects initially; "A subset of all subjects (33 of 58) received a second breath test 1-3 days after initial testing to evaluate the reliability of the BBT." (Methods)
- Reproducibility statistic (Results): "The Kappa value for the agreement between tests was 0.7415 (95% CI: 0.4664-1.0166). The BBT value of the two tests was on average within 10% of each other (95% CI: 7.5-9.8%)." No CV, correlation coefficient, or SD is reported. (Note: the printed CI wording is internally inconsistent, since the stated upper bound 9.8% is below the "within 10%" point estimate; quoted verbatim.)
- Readout: percent dose recovered per hour (%dose/hr), maximum at 10 or 20 min; normal >42%/h. IRMS analytical precision 0.0001 atom% 13C.
- Factors: 8-hour fast essential (a 1-hour fast produced 4/10 false positives, n=10 substudy); doses 10-50 mg gave equivalent diagnostic results (n=22 dose-ranging), though the 10 mg dose was "just barely detectable above natural 13C variability."

**Sasaki et al. 2023, Medical Science Monitor 29:e940238. PMID 37150974.** One-hour PBT in 49 patients with suspected B12 deficiency; 1 g 13C-propionate; infrared isotope spectrometry. Diagnostic study; no repeatability CV. Concluded 1 h is sufficient collection time.

**Sharma/Kumar et al. 2025, Scientific Reports, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-09110-y.** [13C]-propionate oxidation breath test in low-B12 adult males; PDR increased after a single 3 mg IM hydroxocobalamin dose, "in line with earlier findings by Wagner et al. that demonstrated a ~23% change in PDR after treatment."

Earlier IV radiolabel and oral 13C work (Thompson et al. 1989/1990; Walter et al. 1989; Bain et al. 1988) established in vivo propionate oxidation as a severity marker but did not report within-subject repeatability CVs.

### Topic 2: Repeatability of other liver-function 13C breath tests

**13C-methacetin (MBT):**
- Kasicka-Jonderko et al. 2011, World J Gastroenterol 17:4979-4986. DOI 10.3748/wjg.v17.i45.4979; PMID 22174547; PMC3236581. 20 healthy volunteers (10 F, 10 M), 75 mg oral, 3 sessions; short-term paired (median 2 d, range 2-4), medium-term (median 18 d, range 17-23). CVp for AUC0-60 = 10.00% (identical short and medium term); Dmax CVp 16.23%/16.46%; Tmax CVp 30.57%/32.49%. Fixed bias: AUC0-60 rose 7.5% on repeat at 2-3 weeks (persistent CYP1A2 stimulation; 20.80 ± 0.99% vs 22.27 ± 0.98% dose, P=0.017), but no change at the 2-day interval. Repeatability coefficient for AUC0-60 = 5.84-5.86% dose. (Results, Table 1)
- Lalazar et al. 2008, BreathID continuous MBT, PMC2658713. 42 healthy + 11 patients, 2-6 repeats each; inter-test variability ≤13% for PDR peak height (95% CI 0.11-0.15).
- LiMAx (IV 13C-methacetin 2 mg/kg), Lock et al. 2014/Malinowski, J Surg Res, S0022480414007069. 86 healthy; 24 retested the following day. Normal range 430 ± 86 µg/kg/h (lower limit 311-315). ICC 0.85 (95% CI 0.69-0.93) controls; 0.81 (0.60-0.92) in general-anesthesia surgery patients. No effect of age, sex, or BMI. Note: next-day, not same-day.
- Systemic CO2 kinetics: 2DOB/PLOS One and Arch Toxicol 2020 work (PMC3744534) shows individual CO2 kinetics can cause up to 30% (CV ≈ 0.2) deviation in DOB curve parameters at identical drug metabolization rates, i.e., a floor on single-DOB precision that VCO2 normalization or 2-compartment modeling is meant to remove.

**13C-aminopyrine (ABT):**
- Afolabi et al. 2011, Isotopes Environ Health Stud 47(3), DOI 10.1080/10256016.2011.595791. 13C-ABT on 3 occasions in 10 healthy volunteers, single occasion in 22 cirrhotics. Within-subject variability of cPDR (measured VCO2) 15% over 120 min; shortening the test to 30 or 60 min worsened it; predicted VCO2 gave comparable values. "The ABT demonstrates clinically acceptable reproducibility."
- Afolabi et al. 2013, PMID 23799253: 25 cirrhotics vs 18 healthy, cPDR at 2 h median 1.7% vs 9.0% (p<.0001).
- Older methodology: in cirrhotics a repeat 13C-aminopyrine breath test can be performed 3 days after the previous one once label clears (PMID 10690614).

**13C-alpha-ketoisocaproate (KICA):**
- Kasicka-Jonderko et al. 2007, Dig Dis Sci 52:3481-3487; PMID 17393327. 14 healthy, 1 mg/kg + L-leucine, 3 days (short-term ~3.5 d, medium-term ~22 d); cumulative recovery 0-90 to 0-120 min most reproducible; Tmax unsatisfactory; Dmax fairly good.
- Second KICA study, PMID 30880467. 13 healthy adults, 2 occasions ~30 days apart; cumulative 13C-dose enrichment over 60 min had the lowest within-subject variability at 12%; Bland-Altman showed no fixed or proportional bias.

**13C-phenylalanine (PheBT):**
- Kasicka-Jonderko et al. 2018, Isotopes Environ Health Stud 54(3); PMID 29409350. 21 healthy, 100 mg L-[1-13C]phenylalanine, 3 days; AUC most reproducible, but "reproducibility of the 13C-PheBT is worse than either that of the 13C-methacetin or the 13C-KICA breath tests," which "raises concerns whether the precision... may be sufficient to yield clinically sound conclusions."
- Haitjema et al. 2026, Mol Genet Metab 148(4):110183; PMID 42284754. Adults with PAH deficiency, test-retest under stable conditions: AUC ICC 0.93 (95% CI 0.71-0.98); maximum 13C-Phe oxidation ICC 0.91 (0.68-0.98). Good-to-excellent.
- PKU BH4-responsiveness (Pediatr Res 2004, PMID 15319459): cumulative recovery rate controls 15.4 ± 1.5%, heterozygotes 10.3 ± 1.0%, mild HPA 2.74%, mild PKU 1.13 ± 0.14%, classical PKU 0.29 ± 0.14%.
- Ishii et al. 2003 (J Surg Res, PMID 12873431): L-[1-13C]phenylalanine breath test reflects whole-liver PAH activity (15 cirrhotics vs 10 normal-liver), using assumed VCO2 of 300 mmol/m2/h.

**13C-octanoate / 13C-acetate (gastric-emptying comparators, useful as precision references):**
- Choi et al. 1997, Gastroenterology 112:1155-1162, PMID 9097998. 15 healthy, 3 repeats over 3 weeks vs scintigraphy; mean within-individual CV 12% despite wide between-method scatter.
- Ziegler et al. 1996, PMID 8817107. Day-to-day CV in 10 healthy: 29.6% for t1/2breath, 7.4% for gastric emptying coefficient, 46.5% for lag phase.
- Hauser et al. 2006, 19 children, 2-7 days apart; CV of t1/2 13.3 ± 7.9% (range 2.5-29.6%), tlag 13.6 ± 7.5%.
- Perri/13C-acetate infants (PMID 15797668): 14 healthy infants, 2 consecutive days; intraindividual CV 6.3%; 13C-acetate in children (PMID 16641577) median CV of t1/2 8.3%.

### Topic 3: Factors affecting repeatability
- Fasting: an 8-hour fast was essential for the propionate B12 test (Wagner); Manoli used a minimum 3-hour fast. Diet with natural 13C-rich foods (maize, cane sugar, pineapple, kiwi) must be avoided for ~48 h.
- VCO2 normalisation: Manoli measured VCO2 by metabolic cart because predicted equations (the 300 mmol/h/m2 body surface area assumption) were inaccurate in MMA, where resting energy expenditure is lower than predicted; variable VCO2 between visits (e.g., 146.0 to 159.2 ml/min in one subject) was cited as a cause of high patient CV. Afolabi found predicted VCO2 gave cPDR comparable to measured in healthy/cirrhotic adults.
- 13C-bicarbonate recovery fraction: fed-state recovery ~0.81; ~0.70-0.82 depending on feeding state and rest vs exercise (Leijssen & Elia 1996, Clin Sci 91:665-677; physiology infusion studies). Recovery rises with CO2 production and O2 uptake, a source of between-condition variability. 13C and 14C recoveries are essentially bioequivalent (Fuller et al. 2000, PMID 10967605).
- Repeat-dose enzyme induction: methacetin AUC0-60 showed a +7.5% fixed bias at 2-3 weeks (persistent CYP1A2 stimulation); no such effect in propionate data.
- Renal function: in MMA, worsening eGFR between visits (e.g., 57 to 43 mL/min/1.73m2) drove the highest individual CVs; propionate oxidation correlated positively with eGFR (r=0.436, P=0.0098).
- Analytical: IRMS precision ~0.0001 atom% 13C; IRMS and NDIRS (BreathID) agree closely (Bland-Altman LoA -2.4% to +3.3%). Smoking acutely interferes with the methacetin test (Kasicka-Jonderko 2011).

### Topic 4: Tracking enzyme-activity change after therapy
- Liver / combined liver-kidney transplant in MMA: propionate oxidation fully restored to control levels (Manoli 2021; 3 LT/LKT and 3 KT tested pre/post). Isolated kidney transplant gave a smaller change; a failed auxiliary liver allograft showed minimal oxidation.
- B12 therapy, cblA MMA (Manoli 2021): increased propionate oxidation, serum MMA fell from 68 to 14.84 µmol/L.
- Aminopyrine + methionine after liver transplant (Di Campli et al. 2003, Eur J Gastroenterol Hepatol 15:727-732, PMID 12811302): 23 patients, tests on post-op days 1-6; cumulative 13C dose rose progressively to near-control (methionine by day 5) in successful grafts, stayed low in the 2 primary-non-function grafts. Earlier work (Petrolati 2003; Mion et al.; Adolf 1992 14C-ABT) similarly tracked graft recovery/rejection.
- Erythromycin breath test + rifampicin (Gharaibeh et al. 1998, J Clin Pharmacol 38:492-495, PMID 9650537): in 8 healthy male volunteers given 600 mg/day rifampin for 8 days, "Rifampin induced CYP3A, reflected in a mean percent (± standard deviation) increase in EBT values of 86 ± 30%," reversing after 17 days off drug. A clean demonstration that a breath test can quantify an induced enzyme-activity change, with the induction effect (86%) far larger than the ~10-15% test noise. (Two pre-rifampin EBTs 5 days apart were "highly reproducible.")
- 13C-phenylalanine + sapropterin in PKU (Al-Dirbashi/Chakrapani et al., Mol Genet Metab 2015, PMID 25943030): 9 children tested before and after 1 week; "Following sapropterin supplementation for a week, production of 13CO2 significantly increased in five children with a subsequent decline in blood phenylalanine levels... Sapropterin treatment was not effective in three children whose 13CO2 production remained unchanged."
- Moderna mRNA-3927 for propionic acidemia (Nature 2024, PMID 38570682; NCT04159103 / NCT05130437): 16 participants, 346 IV doses over 15.69 person-years; "Preliminary analysis suggests... a 70% reduction in the risk of metabolic decompensation events among 8 participants who reported them in the 12-month pretreatment period." Efficacy was read out via plasma biomarkers (2-MC, 3-HP, C3, n-PG), not a published propionate breath test, so there is no direct effect-size precedent for the propionate breath test as an mRNA/gene-therapy response biomarker outside transplant and B12 repletion.

### Quantitative interpretation for the 12% question (calculations derived by the researcher; arithmetic shown)
Using the healthy-control within-subject CV of 6.9% (Manoli 2021, Results):
- Repeatability coefficient (smallest change detectable in one individual from a single pre vs post test) ≈ 2.77 × within-subject SD = 2.77 × 6.9% ≈ 19%. A 12% change is therefore NOT reliably detectable from a single pair of tests in one person.
- Averaging replicate tests reduces the effective CV by √k: two tests per timepoint give CV ≈ 6.9/√2 ≈ 4.9% and a detectable change ≈ 2.77 × 4.9 ÷ √2... (more directly, RC for a two-vs-two comparison ≈ 2.77 × 6.9/√2 ≈ 13.5%); three tests give CV ≈ 4.0% and a detectable change ≈ 11%.
- For a paired group study (alpha 0.05 two-sided, power 80%): n ≈ (1.96+0.84)^2 × 2 × CV_w^2 / δ^2 = 7.84 × 2 × 6.9^2 / 12^2 = 7.84 × 2 × 47.6 / 144 ≈ 5.2, i.e., about 6 subjects to detect a 12% mean change if within-subject CV is ~6.9%. If the relevant population has the patient CV of ~14%, the same calculation gives 7.84 × 2 × 196 / 144 ≈ 21 subjects.
- These estimates assume the change is real and stable and that VCO2, fasting, dose delivery, and renal status are controlled between tests.

## Recommendations
1. Treat ~12% as detectable only with design safeguards. For an individual-level pharmacodynamic readout (e.g., a single gene-therapy or transplant patient), plan 2-3 replicate propionate tests at each timepoint to push the effective within-subject CV below ~5% and the smallest detectable individual change to ~11-13%. Threshold that would change this: if replicate within-subject CV exceeds ~5%, a 12% individual change cannot be claimed with confidence from a single pre/post comparison.
2. For a group or cohort readout, power for the population's own CV: about 6 subjects if control-like (CV ~7%), about 20 if patient-like (CV ~14%). Re-estimate once you have your own replicate data, because MMA/PA patient CVs run materially higher than control CVs.
3. Standardize aggressively to keep CV at the low end: fixed fast (>=8 h where clinically safe), 48 h avoidance of 13C-rich foods, measured VCO2 by indirect calorimetry rather than the 300 mmol/h/m2 estimate, same time of day, stable renal function between tests, and cumulative percent dose recovered over 120 min as the primary metric. Do not use peak height (Dmax) or time-to-peak (Tmax), which are far less reproducible (CVp 16% and 30%+ for methacetin).
4. Generate the missing same-day data first. Before relying on the test as a ~12% endpoint, run a dedicated within-day and 1-3 day repeatability study in the actual target population, because no same-day propionate data exist and the true within-day CV (likely lower than the 1-3 week or multi-year figures) is unknown.
5. Use IRMS or a validated NDIRS device (e.g., BreathID) interchangeably, since they agree closely (LoA -2.4% to +3.3%), but do not switch analyzers or summary metrics mid-study.

## Caveats
- No same-day (within-day) propionate repeatability exists. The 6.9% control CV is over 1-3 weeks and the 14% patient CV is over 2 months to 4 years, so the true within-day CV is unknown and plausibly lower.
- The Manoli abstract (MMA CV 12.8%) and Results (14.02 ± 12.86%) disagree; the control figure (6.9% vs 6.94%) agrees. Use the Results figures and note the discrepancy.
- CVs are heavily population-dependent: renal impairment, variable VCO2, and dose-delivery issues pushed individual MMA CVs as high as 50.84%.
- Several comparator "reproducibility" figures conflate short-interval reproducibility with day-to-day or week-to-week variability, and ICC values (LiMAx 0.85; PheBT-PKU 0.91-0.93) are not directly convertible to CV, so they are shown as reported rather than harmonized.
- The mRNA-3927 PA trial did not publish a propionate breath test endpoint, so effect-size precedent for the propionate test as a therapy-response biomarker is limited to transplant (full normalization) and B12 repletion (~23% PDR change).
- Precision-limiting phenomena documented for related tests (up to ~30% DOB deviation from CO2 kinetics; +7.5% repeat-dose CYP1A2 induction for methacetin) illustrate biological/methodological noise floors that any 12%-change claim must clear.
