# Endpoint Model Review: Can a 3-HP → decompensation hazard rule be written for propionic acidemia?

## TL;DR

- **No. The reference concentration does not exist in the public record.** The natural history study (Schwahn et al., *Orphanet J Rare Dis* 2026;21:152; doi:10.1186/s13023-026-04251-3) publishes the GAMM's *structure* and its *relative-risk output* (a 50% lower plasma 3-HP → RR 0.7 for metabolic decompensation events, 95% CI 0.6–0.9, p<0.01)\[1\] but never reports the reference/baseline 3-HP concentration, the fitted coefficient/exponent, or the model equation; the single supplementary file contains only descriptive clinical tables (S1–S4), not model parameters. A decision rule keyed to an absolute 3-HP level therefore cannot be written from the published data.
- **The endpoint literature is consistently "smooth," not "threshold."** Every quantitative marker-to-outcome relationship that has been published for PA (3-HP and C3 vs decompensation; 2-MC, C3, FGF21, GDF15 vs neurocognition and long-term complications) is reported as a continuous association (relative risk per halving, regression slope per log-unit, or dose-response). The only threshold-like figure anywhere is an FGF21 cut-off (>1500 pg/mL) for long-term complications from Molema et al. (*J Inherit Metab Dis* 2018, doi:10.1007/s10545-018-0244-6), which reports a positive predictive value of 0.83, a negative predictive value of 1.00 and an odds ratio of 5.0 for a different endpoint (long-term complications) in a 17-patient cohort (11 PA, 4 MMA, 2 IVA; complications-group median 2556.0 pg/ml vs 287.0 pg/ml without).
- **No management guideline sets a target 3-HP or 2-MC level.** The Forny et al. 2021 first-revision guideline (doi:10.1002/jimd.12370) uses 2-MC and C3 only as qualitative surrogates of metabolic stability and explicitly states there is no strong evidence base for monitoring; it names no numeric target. This confirms the paper's premise that any critical-capacity threshold is stipulated, not measured.

## Key findings

1. **The GAMM specification is public; its numeric calibration is not.** The functional form (a piecewise-exponential additive hazard fit with `pammtools` via REML, with terms for 3-HP concentration, a smooth of time since the previous event, and a smooth participant random effect) is fully described in the Methods. But the three quantities the repository needs — reference concentration, fitted exponent, and full equation — are absent from the main text and from the only supplement.

2. **The published exponent uncertainty is consistent with the repository's "factor of 4.8."** The paper reports only relative risks. Converting the halving-RR to an elasticity exponent b (where RR = 0.5^b) gives b ≈ 0.51 at the point estimate, with 95% CI bounds of b ≈ 0.74 (from RR 0.6) and b ≈ 0.15 (from RR 0.9). The ratio of those bounds is ≈4.8 — i.e. the marker-to-hazard exponent is known only to within a multiplicative factor of ~4.8. This is a derived interpretation, not a published number.

3. **Marker-to-outcome effects are smooth across the whole literature.** See the effect-size table below. The natural history study, the NIH severity-modeling and neurocognitive-biomarker studies, and the Longo review all express relationships as continuous slopes or per-fold changes. No study reports a fitted threshold/breakpoint for 3-HP, 2-MC or C3 against decompensation.

4. **The mRNA-3927 trial did not report a per-participant biomarker-to-MDE relationship.** It reports the two effects separately: an overall 70% relative-risk reduction in MDEs (RR 0.30, 95% CI 0.066–1.315, p=0.0927; 82% among biweekly cohorts, RR 0.18, 95% CI 0.011–2.907, p=0.1832), and biomarker reductions after the third dose "in most patients." The two were not linked patient-by-patient, and the paper states the small sample precludes such analysis.

## Details by question

### Q1 — Full specification of the natural history study's longitudinal hazard model

**Source (VERIFIED — full text opened):** Schwahn BC, Berry GT, Vernon HJ, et al. "Characterizing the frequency of clinical events and assessing biomarkers in propionic acidemia: a natural history study." *Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases* 2026;21:152. doi:10.1186/s13023-026-04251-3; PMID 41803992; PMCID PMC13085267. Funded by Moderna; overall study NCT03484767; 50 participants across 25 North American and European sites; ambispective longitudinal design.\[1\]

**Functional form (VERIFIED, verbatim):** "A generalized additive mixed modeling (GAMM) framework was employed, implemented using the R package 'pammtools' [12]. Model fitting was performed through restricted maximum likelihood estimation. The onset of MDEs was modeled using a hazard function influenced by three primary factors: (1) the concentration of 3-HP, (2) a smoothed term capturing the number of days since the end of the previous MDE (or the start of the observation window or birth), and (3) a smoothed random effect term reflecting individual participant variability. To assess the model's suitability for its intended purpose, visual predictive checks were performed. Two primary outcomes were evaluated: the annualized MDE rate and the proportion of participants with annualized MDE rates of at least one."\[1\]

Reference [12] is the method paper: Bender A, Groll A, Scheipl F. "A generalized additive model approach to time-to-event analysis." *Stat Model.* 2017;18:299–321.\[1\] doi:10.1177/1471082X17748083. This is the piecewise-exponential additive mixed model (PAMM) that `pammtools` implements: the hazard is fit as a Poisson GAMM on split follow-up intervals.

**Covariates:** 3-HP concentration (the marker of interest), a smooth of days since the end of the previous MDE (the baseline hazard / time-since-event term), and a subject-level smooth random effect. No other covariates (age, onset class, transplant) are reported as being in the hazard model, though those stratifiers appear in the descriptive analyses.

**Random effects:** a "smoothed random effect term reflecting individual participant variability"\[1\] — i.e. a per-participant random effect entered as a penalized smooth. No variance component, correlation structure, or basis dimension is reported.

**Result of the model (VERIFIED, verbatim):** "Utilizing the GAMM framework, it was observed that a 50% lower plasma 3-HP concentration was associated with a 30% lower frequency of MDEs (RR, 0.7; 95% CI, 0.6, 0.9; p < 0.01). These results were consistent with the negative binomial regression methodology, which demonstrated that a 50% lower plasma 3-HP concentration was associated with a 20% lower frequency of MDE, though these results were not statistically significant (RR, 0.8; 95% CI, 0.5, 1.2; p = 0.25)."\[1\]

**Reference concentration — NOT FOUND (checked exhaustively).** The reference/baseline plasma 3-HP concentration against which the hazard is scaled is not reported anywhere in the public record. What was checked:
- **Main text** (Methods "Statistical analyses"; Results "Correlation between biomarkers and MDEs"; "Baseline biomarker concentrations"): no numeric plasma 3-HP reference value. Baseline 3-HP appears only as a figure (Fig. 4A, plasma mean 3-HP by age), with no reference value in text.\[1\] The only numeric 3-HP concentration mentioned is in the sample-size justification and refers to **urine**, not plasma: "a sample size of up to 60 participants was justified using statistical estimates derived from 300 simulated distributions for urine 3-HP concentrations (μmol/L)."\[1\]
- **Supplementary material:** the article has exactly one supplementary file — "Supplementary material 1 (DOCX)" = Additional file 1 = 13023_2026_4251_MOESM1_ESM.docx. From the main-text call-outs it contains only descriptive clinical tables: **Table S1** (incidence of ≥1 MDE at follow-up by age), **S2** (MDE signs/symptoms/laboratory findings/triggers by age), **S3** (≥1 MDE incidence by disease onset), **S4** (MDE signs/symptoms/triggers by disease onset). None is described as a GAMM or biomarker-regression parameter table. The DOCX file itself is robots-blocked on every Springer/PMC host and could not be opened; however, all main-text references to it point only to S1–S4, and no model-parameter table is cited anywhere.
- **Data availability statement:** the paper provides no repository/code link for the model.
- **Fitted coefficient / exponent — NOT FOUND.** No log-hazard slope, beta, or exponent for the 3-HP term is published; only the relative-risk summaries above. (The "factor of 4.8" the repository references is reproducible as the ratio of the implied exponent CI bounds — see Key finding 2 — but no exponent is stated in the paper.)
- **Spline/knot detail, explicit equation, variance components — NOT FOUND** in main text or in the cited supplement.

**Conclusion for Q1:** The model's *shape* is public and is itself the paper's proposed replacement for a stipulated threshold — a smooth marker-to-hazard function, exactly the hazard-model approach the repository wants. But it is uncalibrated in the public record: without the reference concentration and the fitted 3-HP term, the RR 0.7 cannot be turned into an absolute-level decision rule. This gap is confirmed, not merely unlocated.

### Q2 — Other studies relating 3-HP, 2-MC or C3 to decompensation, hospitalisation or long-term complications

All quantitative relationships found are **smooth** (continuous slope, per-fold RR, or dose-response). The one threshold-type value is an FGF21 cut-off for a different endpoint (long-term complications), in a small cohort. See the effect-size table.

Additional context worth recording:
- The natural history paper's own supporting citation for 3-HP tracking instability is a single case report (Ryu et al. 2012, doi:10.4097/kjae.2013.65.3.257), quoted as "3-HP concentrations may be up to three times higher in participants with MDEs than those without MDEs."\[1\] This is anecdotal, not a fitted effect size.
- The NIH severity-modeling study (Shchelochkov OA, Manoli I, Juneau P, et al. "Severity modeling of propionic acidemia using clinical and laboratory biomarkers," *Genet Med* 2021;23(8):1534–1542; PMID 34007002; 40 participants, k-means clustering) treats plasma total 2-MC and C3 as continuous severity features and notes they are confounded by renal function (eGFR) and levocarnitine dose\[2\] — a caution directly relevant to any dosing rule keyed to these markers.
- No study reports 2-MC as protective against decompensation; in the natural history study 2-MC had essentially no association with MDE risk (RR 1.0).\[1\]

### Q3 — mRNA-3927 (Moderna) trial: biomarker change vs MDE reduction per participant

**Source (VERIFIED — full text opened):** Koeberl D, Schulze A, Sondheimer N, et al. "Interim analyses of a first-in-human phase 1/2 mRNA trial for propionic acidaemia." *Nature* 2024;628:872–877. doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07266-7; PMID via PMC11156579. Trial PARAMOUNT, NCT04159103 (extension NCT05130437). Data cut 31 May 2023; 16 participants, 5 dose cohorts, 346 IV doses over 15.69 person-years.\[3\]\[4\]

**MDE outcome (VERIFIED, verbatim):** "Across all cohorts in the combined dose-optimization and extension studies, participants had an overall relative risk of MDEs of 0.30 (95% confidence interval (CI), 0.066–1.315) during the treatment period compared to the pretreatment period (P = 0.0927). Among only the dosing cohorts receiving treatment every two weeks, the overall relative risk of MDEs in the treatment period versus the pretreatment period was 0.18 (95% CI, 0.011–2.907; P = 0.1832)." Stated as a 70% reduction overall and 82% among biweekly cohorts.\[4\] Denominator: "8/16 (50.0%) participants experienced one or more MDEs during the pretreatment period, 2/16 (12.5%) during the treatment period of the dose-optimization study and 3/16 (18.8%) during the treatment period of the dose-optimization study and the extension study."\[4\]

**Biomarker outcome (VERIFIED, verbatim):** "Reductions from baseline in disease biomarkers, including 3-HP, 2-MC, propionylcarnitine (C3) and n-propionylglycine (n-PG), were observed after dose 3 with mRNA-3927 in most patients (Extended Data Table 3)." Extended Data Table 3 is titled "Biomarker percentage change from baseline after the third dose."\[4\] (The table's numeric cells are robots-blocked and were not opened; the trial reports biomarker change as group percentage change, not per-participant.)

**Per-participant linkage — NOT FOUND / not reported.** The trial reports the MDE relative-risk reduction and the biomarker reductions as two separate results; it does not report a within-participant regression, correlation, or exposure-response model linking each participant's biomarker change to their change in MDE frequency. The paper explicitly cautions: "the small sample size precludes the possibility of performing statistical analyses to assess the significance of these findings"\[4\] and "only half of the participants (8/16) assessed experienced an MDE before treatment, thereby limiting the subset available to fully assess therapeutic efficacy as measured by MDE reduction."\[4\] A separate population PK/PD program (Attarwala H, Lumley M, Liang M, Ivaturi V, Senn J. "Translational Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Model for mRNA-3927," *Nucleic Acid Ther* 2023;33(2):141–147, doi:10.1089/nat.2022.0036; and a model-informed dose-selection abstract) models the *dose*-to-biomarker response via a direct sigmoidal Imax model with IC50_3-HP 37.5 (SE 12.2) µg/g, IC50_2-MC 21.0 (8.96) µg/g and IC50_C3/C2 32.1 (11.7) µg/g, but links dose to biomarker, not biomarker to MDE. Therefore the specific quantity the repository asks for — MDE reduction as a function of biomarker change per participant — is not available from the trial.

### Q4 — Published clinical target level of 3-HP or 2-MC in PA guidelines

**No numeric target exists.** 

- **Forny et al. 2021 first-revision guideline** (doi:10.1002/jimd.12370; PMID 33595124): 2-MC and FGF21 are described as showing "potential to predict the development of long-term complications,"\[5\] and biochemical markers (C3, C3/C2 ratio, methylmalonic acid, 2-methylcitrate, FGF21) are tracked qualitatively, but the guideline sets no target concentration and states of monitoring generally: "There is no strong evidence base as to when which monitoring investigations are indicated in MMA and PA patients."\[5\] The mRNA trial paraphrases the guideline's status of these markers as: "biochemical metabolite levels (such as 2-MC and C3) are often used as surrogate markers for metabolic stability."\[4\] No threshold is attached.
- **GeneReviews (Propionic Acidemia, Shchelochkov/Venditti):** lists metabolic-control monitoring (plasma ammonia, quantitative acylcarnitine profile, electrolytes/venous blood gas, urinary ketones, plasma lactate and 2-methylcitric acid) to be done "as clinically indicated" — again, no target level.
- **Nutrition management guideline (Jurecki et al. 2019):** targets normal-range plasma amino acids and free carnitine, not 3-HP or 2-MC.

This directly supports the repository paper's premise: the "critical-capacity threshold" is not grounded in any measured, guideline-endorsed marker level. What the marker levels are used for clinically is qualitative trend monitoring, not a quantitative cut-off.

## Recommendations for the repository

1. **State plainly in `06_endpoint_model.md` that the reference concentration is not public.** The claim can be made strongly: it is absent from the main text, from the sole supplementary DOCX (which contains only descriptive Tables S1–S4), and from any data/code availability statement. This is a confirmed absence, not a failed search.
2. **Adopt the paper's GAMM shape as the intended hazard model, but flag it as uncalibrated.** The paper already implements exactly the marker→hazard structure the repository advocates (a smooth hazard in 3-HP with a time-since-event baseline and a participant random effect, via `pammtools`/PAMM). The repository's argument is strengthened, not weakened, by this: the field has moved to a smooth hazard, and no threshold appears anywhere.
3. **Do not attempt to back out an absolute decision rule from RR 0.7.** The implied marker-to-hazard exponent is identified only to within a factor of ~4.8 (elasticity CI ≈ 0.15–0.74), and without a reference concentration the RR is dimensionless with respect to absolute 3-HP. Any dosing-interval rule derived from it would be unfalsifiable.
4. **If an absolute rule is essential, the only route is author/sponsor contact or the trial's forthcoming exposure-response analysis** — request (a) the fitted GAMM object or coefficient and reference level from the Schwahn/Moderna group, and (b) any patient-level biomarker-vs-MDE analysis from PARAMOUNT. Both are identified in the pending-reference table.
5. **Keep the FGF21 >1500 pg/mL figure out of the decompensation rule.** It is a long-term-complication cut-off in 17 patients, not a decompensation threshold, and importing it would reintroduce exactly the stipulated-threshold problem the paper criticises.

## Caveats

- The natural history study is Moderna-funded and was designed in part "to support potential dose selection modelling for mRNA";\[1\] the 3-HP GAMM was a secondary, exploratory analysis, was not powered (the dataset "was not powered for binomial regression analyses"),\[1\] and was limited to baseline biomarker readings "due to small sample sizes and high variability."\[1\]
- The GAMM (RR 0.7, significant) and the negative-binomial (RR 0.8, non-significant) analyses of the same 3-HP relationship disagree on significance; the significant result depends on the modelling choice, which is itself a reason not to hard-code a threshold.
- The mRNA-3927 MDE relative risks are not statistically significant (both CIs cross 1; p=0.09 and p=0.18) and the MDE definition changed mid-study,\[4\] so the "70%/82% reduction" figures are interim and fragile.
- 2-MC and C3 are confounded by renal function and levocarnitine dosing (NIH severity model), which complicates their use as clean pharmacodynamic markers.
- The single supplementary DOCX could not be opened by automated tools (robots-blocked); the conclusion that it lacks model parameters rests on the main-text call-outs, which reference only descriptive Tables S1–S4. A manual download is the one residual check.
