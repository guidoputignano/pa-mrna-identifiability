\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The clinical decision in mRNA enzyme replacement for propionic acidemia is a dosing interval set by a quantity nobody measures: the patient's residual enzymatic
capacity $\theta$.

The trial is already running the experiment that would identify it. The therapy is a
known, exogenous input acting selectively on $\theta$, and it supplies forty-two times
the excitation the marker's own excursions do. What this paper contributes is the set of
design rules that follow, rather than any one precision. Sampling must cover the phase of
the dose cycle: draws taken at the dose interval, or at any multiple of it, cost a factor
of $27$ in precision ($21$ to $33$ across simulated intake records), and moving the
schedule half a day recovers it. The dosing record is a regressor carrying the excitation
and not a nuisance. Intake must be recorded as eaten and to better than about $12\%$,
which is a requirement on the \emph{accuracy} of the record and not on its variability.
No prior on $K_m$ is needed or used.

Those rules are statements about the observable and they hold whatever the patient's
capacity. The capacity estimate does not, and the reason is one line of the balance. The
information the record carries about $\ln\theta$ is exactly the flux through the enzyme,
$\theta\,g(m) = L - k_e m$, so the record's sensitivity to capacity is the capacity's own
share of the load. That share is $85\%$ at normal capacity and $16\%$ at the $5\%$ of
normal that defines the disease, where the elasticity
$\lvert\partial \ln m/\partial\ln\theta\rvert$ has fallen from $0.915$ to $0.173$.
Monthly draws across dose cycles for two years therefore identify $\theta$ only for
capacities roughly $82\%$ to $627\%$ of normal. Propionic acidemia sits about a factor of
$16$ below that band, fifteen times the sampling moves its lower edge by $9\%$, and
across a heterogeneous cohort the band edge is what the reported coverage of just over
half is measuring. What survives in the disease range is the half-saturation $K_m$,
identified to about $6\%$ at every capacity, and an upper bound on $\theta$.

Inside the band the honest figure is an interval rather than a standard error. The $95\%$
profile interval on $\theta$ from one two-year monthly record is a factor of about four
($2.9$ to $5.1$ across records), roughly twice what the Fisher curvature alone implies,
because the likelihood has a long tail along the $\theta$--$K_m$ degeneracy.

Identification is not the binding constraint on the decision in any case: the threshold
$\theta$ would be compared against is stipulated rather than measured, and moves by a
factor of $24$ across its defensible range. The paper therefore ends in a data
specification rather than a dosing rule. Its first tier is one unpublished measurement,
the same-day repeatability of a $1$-$^{13}$C-propionate breath test across a single dose.
Its second is the non-enzymatic clearance $k_e$, which no published source supplies, and
which is worth a factor of $2.1$ at normal capacity and $6.9$ in the disease range.
\end{abstract}
