\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The clinical decision in mRNA enzyme replacement for propionic acidemia is a dosing
interval, and it is set by a quantity nobody measures: the patient's residual enzymatic
capacity. What the decision turns on is not that capacity but the \emph{sign} of its
margin against the patient's own propiogenic load. We ask what a clinical record can
deliver, and answer in the units a protocol is written in.

\textbf{The obstruction is a sampling regime, not a structure.} Under a generic trajectory
the model is globally structurally identifiable --- checked by hand and independently by
\texttt{StructuralIdentifiability.jl} across eight configurations --- so the information is
present in the system. What removes it is the regime a clinic samples in. The marker's own
time constant is about $0.8$~h against a therapeutic half-life of $3.5$~days, a separation
of roughly $10^2$, so monthly draws return points on the quasi-steady locus and never the
transient in which the structural result lives. On that locus the information matrix is
singular at the truth and flat only to first order: the degenerate combination is recovered
through curvature rather than slope, so its recoverable displacement scales as the
\emph{square root} of the noise where every other direction scales linearly. A singular
Fisher matrix at a point does not contradict global injectivity; it sets the price.

What that price looks like in the data is a straight line. Over the band a stable patient
visits, total clearance is indistinguishable from an affine function at assay resolution,
and the capacity survives only in that line's intercept. \textbf{A degeneracy of this kind
closes when an independent channel is known}, and the paper's question is therefore not
whether identification is possible but \emph{which channel it is feasible to know, and to
what precision}.

The obstruction has an exact closed form. At a constant recorded load the three design
columns satisfy $c_\theta q_\theta + c_{K_m} q_{K_m} = q_{L_0}$ with
$c_\theta = (1 + K_m k_e/L)/\theta$ and $c_{K_m} = -1/L$, so the design matrix is rank
deficient by exactly one and the deficient direction is the load bias. The identity names
its own cure: it needs $-1/L$ to be a single constant, so \textbf{it is the day-to-day
\emph{variability} of ordinary eating, not the precision of the dietary record, that lets
the data close the degeneracy rather than a prior.} Recording intake twenty times more
precisely is worth six per cent; a record smoothed to a weekly mean is exactly the singular
case, and the instruction that follows is to omit a data-cleaning step rather than to add
a measurement.

\textbf{The channel that is feasible to know is the therapy itself.} The ratio of the two
sensitivities is $-(1 + \Delta(t)/\theta)\,\varphi(m)$, a product of two factors, and the
literature has measured only the second. On the record the phase 1/2 schedule already
produces, the marker band moves $\varphi$ by five per cent while the dose train moves
$1 + \Delta/\theta$ by a factor of $2.64$: \textbf{the therapy supplies forty-two times the
excitation the marker does, on the same patients, and nearly four times what the decision
needs.} The trial has been administering the excitation and reading it as a nuisance.

What follows is a design, not an excursion. Monthly outpatient sampling across dose cycles
identifies the capacity to $17\%$ over two years, and two-monthly sampling --- thirteen
draws --- to $19\%$, given the non-enzymatic clearance within a factor of three and given
the delivered increment characterised to about $12\%$. Across a plausibly heterogeneous
cohort the median is $0.19$ and \textbf{just over half of patients meet a $20\%$ target},
because the dose is fixed in absolute terms while residual capacity is not.

\textbf{The therapy is not a design improvement; it is the design's condition of existence,
and no second specification is available for untreated patients.} On the same footing,
spontaneous load variability alone gives $1.79$ and adding recorded carnitine steps makes it
\emph{worse} --- carnitine can only move the weak factor of the product, and it moves the
patient deeper into the linear regime to do so, about ten to one against. Reaching the
target without a dose train would need day-to-day intake varying by $229\%$, or daily
venepuncture for two years, which still lands at $0.67$ because information in the
degenerate direction saturates rather than accumulating as $1/\sqrt{n}$. Nothing is assumed about $K_m$: a
prior twenty times looser changes no number here. \textbf{The one schedule that fails is
the obvious one.} Sampling on dosing days, or at any multiple of the dose interval, lands
every draw at the same phase, collapses the range of $\Delta/\theta$ from $1.95$ to
$0.13$, and takes the precision from $0.17$ to $3.51$; moving the schedule half a day
recovers it.

\textbf{Identifiability was not the binding constraint on the decision, and finding the
channel does not repeal that.} Simulated prior-free, the sign of the margin is still wrong
for about two patients in five near the boundary, on the new design as on the old; the
cohort needed to place the affected fraction within ten points falls from roughly $4\,900$
to $2\,200$, which is a real gain and still impossible against a phase 1/2 trial of
sixteen. Degrading the increment to the $12\%$ the identifiability gate asks for moves the
sign error by less than its seed-to-seed spread. The sign of a quantity near its own zero
is hard for reasons that are not about what the record contains.

The decision also inherits an asymmetry that reverses between regimes, and neither half
should be quoted alone. While the record tracks the load, the estimator fails towards
declaring patients safe --- $17\%$ against $42\%$ at equal distance from the boundary.
Under crisis protocol, where prescribed intake is cut while catabolism supplies the load,
the fit has nowhere to put the marker except lost capacity, the estimate collapses by two
orders of magnitude, and the errors invert to $99\%$ against nothing: the same estimator
declares everyone unsafe. Which regime a record came from is a fact about the chart, not
about the patient.

The note ends in a two-tier data specification. Tier~2 is a small prospective outpatient
study. Tier~1 is a single measurement nobody has published --- the same-day repeatability
of a $1$-$^{13}$C-propionate breath test across one dose --- and the honest output of this
analysis is that \textbf{until that number exists, no protocol can be written}. What the
analysis eliminates is what earlier drafts asked for: high-frequency sampling at a fixed
operating point, and decompensation records, which fail twice over because the required
excursion is not observed and because the model loses its stable equilibrium there.
\end{abstract}
