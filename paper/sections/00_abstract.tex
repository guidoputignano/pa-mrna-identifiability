\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The clinical decision in mRNA enzyme replacement for propionic acidemia is a dosing
interval, and it is set by a quantity nobody measures: the patient's residual enzymatic
capacity. What the decision actually turns on is not that capacity but the \emph{sign} of
its margin against the patient's own propiogenic load. We ask what a clinical record can
deliver, and answer in the units a protocol is written in.

Marker levels cannot deliver the capacity. Over the band a stable patient's marker visits,
total clearance is indistinguishable from a straight line at assay resolution, and the
capacity survives only in that line's intercept, which the band reaches by extrapolation.
The residual family is a fibre whose width is $\left(C(\bar m)/a\right)^2$, the recorded
load over the fitted intercept, squared: an observable quantity, $169$ at our illustrative
values. Independently, oxidative capacity assayed by breath test is near-identical in
patients whose serum markers differ widely~\cite{breath_test_mma}. % C48

The decision survives what the parameter does not, but asymmetrically. Simulating the
accessible design, the estimated capacity ranges over three orders of magnitude while the
sign of the margin is right $99.8\%$ of the time for a patient with margin --- and wrong
$53\%$ of the time for a patient without one, at the same distance from the boundary. The
estimator fails towards declaring patients safe.

What would fix it is an excitation condition, and it is not the one a sampling schedule
supplies. Both ends of the saturation curve are degenerate: below the half-saturation level
only a lumped ratio is identifiable, and above it the capacity is confounded with the
unrecorded component of the load, which is the worse failure because it loses the target
rather than a nuisance. Identifiability therefore requires \emph{traversing} the
half-saturation level, and the excitation is maximised when the band straddles it in
geometric mean, at $(\sqrt r - 1)/(\sqrt r + 1)$ for a fold range $r$.

\textbf{Time does not buy this.} Sampling every two days for two years, under the trial's
biweekly schedule, improves the capacity's precision by a tenth of a decade over four clinic
visits; the same number of samples placed to straddle the half-saturation level improves it
by two decades. Of the excursions a record contains, only a decompensation comes within a
factor of two of what the decision requires: \textbf{the excitation is available only from
the event the therapy exists to prevent}. Asked directly, the sign error for a patient
$30\%$ below the threshold falls from $36\%$ at a two-fold excursion to $10\%$ at
twenty-fold, and then stops falling --- the residue is the confounding with the unrecorded
load, which excursion cannot reach. The paper ends in the one-page data specification that
follows from saying so, and the first row of it is the load record.
\end{abstract}
